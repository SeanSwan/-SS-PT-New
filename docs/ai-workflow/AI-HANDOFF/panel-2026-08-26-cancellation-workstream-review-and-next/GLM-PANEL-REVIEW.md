2. **P1 — Money adjustments have no durable audit trail.** `chargeAmountSubmitted` / `chargeAmountSource` are logged but persisted to no column (§3 item 3; §7 Q3). Worse: because `applyServerDerivedChargeAmount` mutates `billingOptions` in place and logs at mutation time, a transaction rollback *after* the log leaves phantom audit entries asserting an adjustment that never committed. **Decision A — column on `StorefrontItem`, or link `Session → OrderItem`?**
Both, sequenced. Add nullable `durationMinutes` to `StorefrontItem` now, with a refuse-to-derive guard when null — one additive migration, unblocks derivation correctness, consistent with the fail-closed philosophy. Build the `Session → OrderItem` link when B forces it, because **expiry cannot be done correctly without lot attribution anyway** — a per-purchase validity window requires knowing which purchase's credit a session consumes. The link is the better long-term shape but the bigger lift (touches the booking/credit-deduction path), and doing it first buys nothing that the nullable column plus guard doesn't already secure. Caution: a denormalized duration column can drift from the funding item once the link exists — document the column as display/derivation convenience, the lot as provenance.

**Decision B — should sessions expire?**
Yes, but softly, and check the law first. Validity = program window + 3 months (a 12-month program's 192 sessions valid 15 months). At expiry, do **not** auto-confiscate: surface the balance to the trainer as an at-risk review queue with one-click extension at trainer discretion, and convert (not void) on true expiry — e.g., rollover into the next purchase at partial value. Rationale: the forfeit already exists as the hard edge; expiry's real value is *urgency to book*, which in a trainer-led B2B2C business operates through the trainer relationship and the backfill priority score, not through a policy engine punishing a client who prepaid thousands. A single-trainer business wants discretion, not enforcement. And verify consumer-protection rules on prepaid service expiry in the operating jurisdiction before writing the policy — that is a real constraint, not a formality. Implementation note: this decision *requires* the A-lot link; that's another reason to sequence A's column now and the link when this is decided.

**Decision D — fee on top of forfeit for client late-cancel?**
No. Forfeiting a prepaid $110–$175 session **is** a 100% late-cancel charge — stronger than industry norm. A cash fee on top double-charges one behavior, requires per-fee payment rails and dispute surface that don't exist for one-off charges, and would reverse the honesty fix by building a punitive fee to make old invented copy true. If a repeat-offender deterrent is ever needed, use backfill-offer priority demotion — money the client already paid is the maximum ethical stick this business needs, and the reschedule-first flow (offer a same-week rebooking before the cancel finalizes) is the better answer to the same problem because it prevents the forfeit entirely.
