# 3. Answers to the ten review questions

## Q1. What is developmentally wrong, unsafe, over-ambitious, or missing?

### Over-ambitious or potentially wrong

- A daily “primary activity” should not imply that an adult-directed product is the center of a two-year-old’s day. A familiar play invitation or care routine may be the appropriate primary experience.
- Five day cards should be five small variations on one or two repeated routines, not five novel performances.
- “Observable signs” can become covert assessment or comparison. They should be framed as optional participation cues that help the adult adapt the environment—not scores, milestones to elicit, or proof that an activity succeeded.
- Weather and seasonal themes must not override children’s current interests, regulation needs, cultural context, or safe routines.
- Sensory activities are presented too casually. For this age they need explicit ingestion, choking, allergy, cross-contact, sanitation, spill, slip, and supervision review.
- “Affordable” used materials may be unsuitable because of missing pieces, recalls, degraded plastic, paint, pests, magnets, unsecured batteries, porous surfaces, or inability to sanitize.

### Missing

- Co-regulation and responsive caregiving.
- Diapering, meals, rest, handwashing, arrival, departure, cleanup, and transitions as learning contexts.
- Outdoor play and opportunities for large movement.
- Process-oriented art rather than teacher-produced crafts.
- Concrete dual-language supports: gestures, home-language words supplied or approved by families, picture choices, and wait time.
- Adaptations that are operational, not generic: seated alternatives, larger handles, reduced sensory load, multiple communication modes, and optional participation.
- Staffing, supervision, room layout, evacuation, sanitation, allergy, and school-material restrictions.
- A rule that developmental milestones are not diagnostic instruments. The CDC’s milestone materials support developmental awareness but do not authorize AI diagnosis: [CDC milestones at two years](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/2-years.html).
- A pathway for concerns: document only necessary observations locally, consult the designated supervisor, and communicate through the school’s approved process.

The overall approach should follow responsive, play-based, individualized practice described by the [California Infant/Toddler Learning and Development publications](https://www.cde.ca.gov/sp/cd/re/cddpublications.asp), [NAEYC Developmentally Appropriate Practice](https://www.naeyc.org/resources/position-statements/dap/contents), and [Head Start Effective Practice Guides](https://headstart.gov/school-readiness/effective-practice-guides/effective-practice-guides).

## Q2. What should the daily and weekly experience look like?

### Daily: one local card, normally under 90 seconds to review

**Top line**

- Today’s familiar rhythm.
- One likely pressure point expressed cautiously: “Rain may reduce outdoor movement,” not a prediction about children.
- An explicit offline/source freshness indicator.

**One invitation**

- What to place out.
- Two-minute setup.
- Up to three materials.
- A no-purchase substitute.
- One sentence explaining why it is suitable for many two-year-olds.

**Adult actions**

- Three short language prompts.
- One model-and-wait strategy.
- One adaptation for communication, mobility, and sensory participation.
- One movement option and one calm transition.

**Safety strip**

- Choking/ingestion.
- Allergy/cross-contact.
- Sanitation/cleanability.
- Supervision and room-layout concern.
- “Do not use” condition where applicable.

**Teacher decisions**

- **Use**
- **Swap**
- **Skip**

No explanation is required.

**End of day**

- One tap: used / adapted / skipped.
- Optional second tap: engaged / mixed / not today / setup too hard.
- Optional short local text note.
- Voice input remains disabled by default because it could capture children or family information.

No daily shopping lead should be shown unless it directly serves an approved need. Bargains should not create artificial urgency.

### Weekly: approximately 15 minutes

1. **Three minutes — Confirm constraints**
   - Calendar and closures.
   - One teacher-selected theme or current child interest.
   - Available materials.
   - Known policy or safety restrictions.

2. **Eight minutes — Approve a repeated core**
   - One book, song, or language routine.
   - One manipulatives/play invitation.
   - One movement pattern.
   - Small variations across five days.
   - Care-routine and self-help opportunities.
   - Concrete accessibility options.

3. **Three minutes — Supplies**
   - Must-have.
   - Already owned.
   - Safe substitute.
   - Borrow/free.
   - Optional purchase.

4. **One minute — Commit**
   - Approve cards locally.
   - Export or print only if needed.
   - Nothing is sent to families, calendars, stores, or cloud services automatically.

## Q4. How can radar find local ideas and bargains compliantly?

### Preferred source order

1. Government and authoritative education sources.
2. Public libraries, parks, museums, reuse organizations, and community organizations.
3. School-provided approved-vendor lists.
4. Manufacturer product pages and manuals.
5. Public merchant pages, newsletters, and authorized feeds.
6. Professional early-childhood organizations.
7. Teacher blogs and social content for inspiration only, never as sole support for safety or developmental claims.
8. Marketplace listings supplied or opened by a human.

### Marketplace-compliant workflow

- Teacher creates native saved searches and notifications.
- Radar proposes search phrases but does not execute prohibited collection.
- Email notifications are ingested only if the platform’s current terms and the school’s email policy permit it.
- Teacher opens the native app or website.
- Teacher manually chooses **Save to SwanGuard** or pastes a listing link.
- Radar scores that supplied link for likely age suitability, completeness, cleaning burden, price, distance, and safety uncertainty.
- Teacher independently verifies current listing, stock, seller, pickup location, and condition.
- Contact, negotiation, reservation, payment, and purchase always require a direct human action.

No scraping, CAPTCHA evasion, proxy rotation, shared accounts, autonomous messages, or automated purchase should be implemented. Current terms must be rechecked at launch: [Meta automated data collection terms](https://www.facebook.com/legal/automated_data_collection_terms), [OfferUp terms](https://offerup.com/terms), and [Craigslist terms](https://www.craigslist.org/about/terms).

Local availability must be labeled **unverified until opened by the teacher**, with retrieval time and source URL. Do not claim a nearby store has stock unless the merchant itself shows current stock and the teacher confirms it.

## Q5. What inventory, source-quality, safety, and recall checks are required?

Every recommendation should contain:

### Product identity and cost

- Product name, manufacturer, exact model/SKU when available.
- New/used/unknown condition.
- Quantity and dimensions.
- Unit price and estimated total.
- Shipping, tax, membership, or pickup costs marked unknown unless directly sourced.
- URL, retrieval timestamp, and source type.
- Stock/availability confidence.

### Suitability

- Manufacturer’s age grading—not an AI-inferred age.
- Intended use and expected supervision.
- Number of children the quantity supports.
- Required setup and storage.
- Accessible alternatives.
- Whether a no-purchase substitute exists.

### Safety

- Small or detachable parts and choking concerns.
- Magnets, button/coin batteries, cords, sharp points, pinch points, tip-over risk, projectile risk, and entrapment openings.
- Ingestion and allergy/cross-contact risks.
- Material/paint uncertainty.
- Washability, disinfection compatibility, drying needs, and porous surfaces.
- Broken, cracked, degraded, sticky, rusted, or missing components.
- Missing labels, instructions, guards, battery covers, or fasteners.
- Fabric pest, mold, smoke, and fragrance concerns.
- Whether the school permits the category.

Use the [CPSC recall database](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls) and the CPSC’s [small-parts guidance](https://www.cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Business-Education/Business-Guidance/Small-Parts-for-Toys-and-Childrens-Products). A recall search is not proof of safety; it is one required check.

### Used-item rule

Reject or escalate any item when:

- Exact identity cannot be established for a category where recall matching matters.
- It is recalled, broken, incomplete, structurally modified, or missing safety hardware.
- It cannot be reliably cleaned.
- It contains accessible strong magnets or an insecure battery compartment.
- Paint/material condition is uncertain and exposure is plausible.
- A seller photo is insufficient to assess condition.
- School procurement or sanitation policy disallows it.

Opened consumables, food-contact items, and porous sensory materials should default to reject unless an applicable school policy expressly permits them.

## Q7. Is Qwen 3.8 27B a sensible default on 24 GB and 32 GB Macs?

**No as a cross-device default.**

- **24 GB:** Not a sensible default based on the packet’s own approximately 19 GB model-size estimate before context, runtime, and operating-system overhead. Installation might technically succeed, but comfortable interactive use is not demonstrated.
- **32 GB:** A test candidate, not an automatic default. It has more headroom, but acceptable context size, latency, thermals, multitasking behavior, and stability must be measured on the actual Mac.
- The exact designation **“Qwen 3.8 27B”**, publisher model card, license, quantization provenance, runtime compatibility, and artifact digest are not established by the packet. Verify them upstream before installation.

### Explicit alternatives

These are candidate model families/classes, not asserted Ollama tags:

- **24 GB:** Approved 7–9B instruct model, such as a current Qwen3 8B-class or Llama 3.1 8B-class artifact if its license, model card, exact artifact, and Hermes/Ollama compatibility are verified.
- **32 GB:** Approved 12–14B instruct model, such as a current Qwen3 14B-class or Gemma 3 12B-class artifact after the same verification.
- **Either Mac:** Template-and-retrieval mode with no generative model. This is the most reliable offline fallback.
- **Online but private:** Use the 27B-class private server for generic planning when available, with visible status and no silent fallback.

Tradeoff: smaller models are more likely to preserve interactive headroom but may need stronger templates, retrieval, and teacher review. The 27B-class model may improve complex drafting, but fit and responsiveness are unproven. No hardware performance figures should be promised before a local benchmark.

Acceptance should be based on teacher tasks: startup, one-card generation, source use, correction following, memory pressure, concurrent normal applications, and successful operation after network loss.

# 5. Pedagogy and toilet-learning corrections

## Teaching doctrine

Use the following operational sequence:

1. Observe without assigning intent.
2. Consider more than one explanation.
3. Adjust environment, timing, language, materials, or adult support.
4. Allow non-participation.
5. Document only what the program requires.
6. Escalate concerns through the human program process—not to an AI diagnostician.

Embedded opportunities should be brief and occur within play and routines, consistent with the [Head Start embedded learning opportunities guidance](https://headstart.gov/publication/embedded-learning-opportunities-faqs).

Plans should:

- Repeat songs, books, gestures, and play schemas.
- Use simple language without testing children for a response.
- Offer showing, pointing, moving, vocalizing, or watching as valid participation.
- Avoid forced circle participation and product-identical crafts.
- Treat milestones and the [California DRDP](https://www.cde.ca.gov/SP/CD/ci/desiredresults.asp) as program-specific developmental frameworks, not daily grading engines.
- Follow applicable guidance specifically for this age group, including the [California FAQ for serving two-year-olds](https://www.cde.ca.gov/sp/cd/ci/serving2yofaqs.asp).

## Toilet learning

The packet’s non-coercive direction is correct but needs operational safeguards:

- Readiness is individualized; age alone is not a gate.
- Coordinate with the authorized family representative and the program’s written policy.
- Continue diapering and normal care without portraying it as failure.
- Offer predictable opportunities briefly, without forced or prolonged sitting.
- Use neutral terms for bodies, urine, bowel movements, and accidents.
- Preserve privacy; no public charts or peer comparison.
- Give calm, specific encouragement for communication and participation, not promises or pressure.
- Make clothing and physical access manageable.
- Accidents are care events, not misconduct.
- Do not infer defiance, developmental disorder, or medical cause.
- Pain, constipation, bleeding, urinary symptoms, or substantial regression require the established family/medical escalation pathway.
- Do not promise that a method will produce dryness by a date.

These principles align with the American Academy of Pediatrics’ emphasis on readiness and avoiding pressure: [AAP/HealthyChildren toilet-learning readiness](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/toilet-training/Pages/the-right-age-to-toilet-train.aspx).

AI may prepare a neutral routine card for the adult. It should not speak directly to a child about toileting, deliver rewards, track public success, or replace adult-family coordination.

- Two consecutive clean fresh-vantage hostile rounds occur after repairs.
- Evidence is reproducible.

Passing this gate still does not permit child-specific content to reach external providers.

## Rollback

- Export only approved local settings and generic templates.
- Maintain a known-good installer/version where licensing permits.
- Stop scheduled jobs and launch agents.
- Revoke SwanGuard, private-server, and any cloud credentials.
- Remove firewall/overlay permissions.
- Archive or securely delete the Hermes home according to approved retention policy.
- Restore from a tested encrypted backup.
- Verify that old credentials and devices cannot reconnect.
- Document which version, policy, and model digest were restored.

# 7. Privacy attack paths and required tests

| Attack path | Required test |
|---|---|
| Direct provider SDK bypass | Remove broker route and confirm every provider call fails |
| Browser or plugin egress | Inject canary into browser/tool context; verify no outbound transmission |
| DNS, IPv6, alternate port, QUIC, or tunnel bypass | Network capture and deny tests across all interfaces |
| Cloud fallback after local error | Force model timeout/OOM/unavailable state; confirm visible local failure |
| OCR and attachment leak | Test PDF, image, HEIC, DOCX, ZIP, multipart, unsupported and malformed files |
| Audio/transcription leak | Synthetic background names and family details; confirm local block |
| Conversation-history leak | Place secret in prior turn and send safe final turn; verify exclusion |
| Tool-result callback leak | Put canary in tool output, error, stack trace, and streamed result |
| Unicode and obfuscation | Homoglyphs, zero-width characters, RTL text, misspellings, nicknames, emoji and encoding changes |
| Structured-data bypass | Nested JSON, arrays, escaped strings, base64, XML, YAML and multipart fields |
| Semantic re-identification | Unique incidents, rare disability combinations, exact schedules, sibling/family references |
| Logging/telemetry leak | Inspect application, OS, crash, proxy, server and broker logs |
| Cache/embedding leak | Verify no remote embeddings, prompt cache, history sync, or remote vector database |
| Radar prompt injection | Pages that order the agent to expose files, reveal tokens, message sellers, or ignore policy |
| Malicious redirects/tracking URLs | Redirect-chain and query-string exfiltration tests |
| Race and streaming leak | Secret appears after initial approval or across stream chunks |
| Policy unavailable/stale | Corrupt, remove, or downgrade policy; request must block |
| Roster unavailable | Remove roster/classification source; request must block |
| Sanitizer crash/timeout | Process must deny, not pass original input |
| Unknown content type | Deny without attempting external upload |
| Credential contamination | Place token in prompt, file metadata, URL, clipboard, and environment variable |
| Backup leakage | Restore and inspect backups; confirm encryption, access and deletion behavior |
| Private-server compromise | Verify what prompts, caches, swap, logs, and backups an administrator could recover |
| Insider misuse | Test that separate identities produce attributable audit records and can be independently revoked |

No test result justifies a “100% safe” statement.

# 8. Adoption risks and simplifications

| Risk | Simplification |
|---|---|
| Too many daily recommendations | Show one invitation; hide alternatives until requested |
| Planning feels like another assessment task | No child scoring or required narrative |
| Inventory becomes stale | Track only durable high-use materials and teacher-confirmed shortages |
| Bargains create urgency | Weekly opportunity queue; at most one optional lead daily |
| Teacher must learn prompting | Use fixed cards and plain-language controls |
| Connectivity becomes dependency | Offline templates, local source cache, print view |
| Local model is slow or unreliable | Smaller model or deterministic template mode |
| Safety warnings become boilerplate | Display only applicable checks plus a fixed “adult verifies” rule |
| Weekly plans become five spectacles | Repeat a core routine with small changes |
| Feedback is burdensome | One or two taps; optional text |
| AI crowds out interaction | Screen remains teacher-facing and closes after preparation |
| Source confidence is ignored | Put source class and freshness next to the suggestion, not in a hidden panel |

A realistic pilot should compare planning time, skipped recommendations, unsafe or unusable suggestions, and teacher-reported burden against the current manual process. Do not measure success by number of generated activities.

# 9. Single highest-risk assumption and fastest test

**Highest-risk assumption:** that every possible outbound path can be forced through one fail-closed broker.

**Fastest useful test:**

1. Use a dedicated test Mac with synthetic canary child records.
2. Deny all internet egress except the proposed broker destination.
3. Exercise Hermes, every skill, browser, OCR, transcription, attachments, private-server client, retries, telemetry, callbacks, and malformed inputs.
4. Attempt DNS, IPv6, alternate-port, redirect, streaming, and direct-SDK bypasses.
5. Capture traffic independently of the application.
6. Confirm that every canary remains local and every broker failure blocks.
7. Repair findings, then commission two fresh-vantage hostile rounds.

Until that succeeds reproducibly, external reviewing remains disabled. The broker is not proven today.
