## Section 10 — answers

### 1. What is developmentally wrong, unsafe, over-ambitious, or missing?

**Wrong / over-ambitious**
- Treating two-year-olds as short preschoolers: distinct “primary activities,” themed productions, forced group performance, academic sorting/matching “lessons,” and turn-taking as an expected group skill. At ~24 months, parallel play dominates; turn-taking and sustained pretend are emerging, not scheduled outcomes.
- Using CDC 2-year milestones as teaching targets or “observable signs” that the AI (or teacher via AI) checks off. CDC’s own frame is *Learn the Signs. Act Early* screening, not a curriculum. Source: https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/2-years.html
- Five unrelated weekly spectacles. Infant/toddler doctrine is repeated routines with small variations and embedded learning, not a new show daily. Head Start ELO: https://headstart.gov/publication/embedded-learning-opportunities-faqs
- Circle times longer than a song, fingerplay, or book-on-the-lap. Two-year-olds vote with their bodies.
- Any output that turns developmental variation into delay language, “red flags” as diagnosis, or behavior ranking.

**Unsafe if generated naively**
- Sensory/food play without allergy, ingestion, choking, and sanitation gates (flour, salt dough, latex, essential oils, cinnamon, glitter, water beads, kinetic sand, balloons, plastic bags, button batteries, loose magnets, broken used toys).
- Water play: drowning risk exists in inches; this is supervision policy, not an AI activity card.
- Used items: lead paint, recalls, bedbugs/fabric pests, cracked plastic, missing eyes/squeakers, tip-over furniture, non-cleanable plush that will be mouthed.
- Fine-motor “loose parts” that fail a choke-tube test (US small-parts cylinder, 1.25 in / 31.7 mm — verify against CPSC 16 CFR 1501; do not invent a classroom exception).
- AI-suggested toileting schedules, prolonged sitting, or public accident tracking.

**Missing from the packet**
- Serve-and-return, primary caregiving, and following the child’s lead as first-class design, not flavor text. NAEYC DAP: https://www.naeyc.org/resources/position-statements/dap/contents
- Outdoor play as a daily default, not a “movement option.”
- The same 3–5 books and the same songs all week (repetition is the pedagogy).
- Rest/nap, biting (modal 2-year-old group issue), separation, dual-language input, family toileting culture, mixed-age vs. 2s-only, and the program’s *existing* mandated curriculum (do not overlay a second curriculum).
- Explicit choke, recall, non-toxic art (ASTM D-4236 / AP), and “will this be mouthed?” checks on every material line.
- A paper/offline floor when Mac, GPU, or radar is dead.
- Screen discipline: teacher glances at a card in planning time; the device is not in the children’s play space as a co-teacher.

California anchors to prefer over Pinterest-logic: Infant/Toddler Foundations and framework https://www.cde.ca.gov/sp/cd/re/cddpublications.asp ; serving two-year-olds https://www.cde.ca.gov/sp/cd/ci/serving2yofaqs.asp ; DRDP as *program* continuum, not an AI grader https://www.cde.ca.gov/SP/CD/ci/desiredresults.asp.

### 2. What should daily and weekly teacher experience actually look like?

**Daily, ≤90–180 seconds, local/offline first, no shopping.**
- Today’s rhythm already known (arrival → play → snack → outdoor → lunch → rest → play → goodbye) plus *one* likely pressure point (e.g., late morning hunger, first rain, substitute).
- One play invitation using materials already in the room (setup ≤2 minutes).
- One movement/outdoor default and one calm transition (song/cue), not a new curriculum block.
- Safety line: choking/allergy/water/sun/sanitation *for today’s invitation only*.
- “Why this fits two-year-olds” in one sentence, with source class (CDE/NAEYC/Head Start vs generated).
- Three taps later: used / adapted / skipped. Optional: setup-too-hard.
- If the model is down: show yesterday’s cached card and the standing routine. That is success.

**Weekly, ≤15–20 minutes, one skeleton, not five shows.**
- Keep the same daily skeleton. Choose one thread (weather, a book already owned, a material already on the shelf).
- Five day cards = five small variations of the same invitations (new song verse, same blocks plus scarves, outdoor if weather allows).
- Language prompts as adult scripts (“you poured,” “you did it”), not child performance goals.
- Adaptations as a standing checklist (communication, mobility, sensory, dual-language, “opt out without shame”), not five parallel IEPs invented by a model.
- Inventory: must-have consumables / substitute / borrow-or-free / optional. Shopping list capped (e.g., ≤3 items) and never in the morning briefing.
- Feedback in, next week simpler.

AI prepares the adult before children arrive. During class, the adult is with children.

## Two-year-old pedagogy and toilet-learning corrections (with citations)

**Pedagogy corrections**
- Anchor to CA Infant/Toddler Foundations (relationships, language, cognitive, perceptual/motor), not TK/K units: https://www.cde.ca.gov/sp/cd/re/cddpublications.asp and two-year-old program guidance https://www.cde.ca.gov/sp/cd/ci/serving2yofaqs.asp
- NAEYC DAP: age, individual, and cultural appropriateness; play and relationships are the curriculum: https://www.naeyc.org/resources/position-statements/dap/contents
- Head Start: embed instruction in routines and play, not add-on lessons: https://headstart.gov/school-readiness/effective-practice-guides/effective-practice-guides and https://headstart.gov/publication/embedded-learning-opportunities-faqs
- CDC 2-year list is a screener for families/clinicians, not a lesson map: https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/2-years.html
- DRDP is an observation continuum for programs, not an AI scoring engine: https://www.cde.ca.gov/SP/CD/ci/desiredresults.asp
- Doctrine to encode in every card: joyful, strengths-based, short, inside real care routines; simple language; modeling; repetition; choices; no shame; no forced group performance; observe → interpret cautiously → adapt environment → document only what policy requires.
- Screen output is for the adult in planning time. It does not replace serve-and-return.

**Toilet-learning corrections**
- AAP: there is no single right age; wait for readiness (body awareness, communication, motor, interest); do not start because the calendar says 2; no punishment, no shaming, no battles: https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/toilet-training/Pages/the-right-age-to-toilet-train.aspx
- In group care, coordinate with the authorized representative **and** the school’s written plan. AI does not set a classroom-wide “we toilet-train this month.”
- Adult-prep only: easy clothing, predictable opportunities, neutral words, modeling, sanitation, supervision. Never force prolonged sitting, never treat accidents as misconduct, never promise medical outcomes, never public charts.
- Accidents: comfort + hygiene + policy documentation. Not model training data.
- Licensing/supervision/bathroom-visibility rules: **unverified for this site** — director/Title 22, not the model.
