# FOUR NEW ANSWERS — FINAL (amendments folded) + ENTERPRISE ROADMAP

## 11 — Head impact
He's done for the day — not negotiable, not about how he feels: a knock to the head plus ANY change ends the session, and slowed answers are a change. No finishing sets, no same-day return no matter how fine he feels in an hour. Because he's already showing a mental-status change, medical evaluation is NOW — not an end-of-day appointment. He does not drive himself home under any circumstances, and he is not left alone today — someone stays with him and knows this list. Emergency services NOW if: worsening headache, repeated vomiting, confusion deepening, slurred speech, unequal pupils, increasing drowsiness or he can't be woken, loss of consciousness at any point, or a seizure. Return to training only per medical clearance and a graded return — not my protocol to invent [verify concussion consensus statement]. Incident documented with mechanism, time, and what I observed.

## 12 — Suspected rhabdomyolysis
No — do not "hydrate and stretch it out." That combination — soreness way beyond normal, swelling with lost range, and dark brown urine — needs same-day medical care, today, and I tell him exactly that without diagnosing: "this is beyond soreness, it needs to be looked at today." If he can't be seen today, urgent care or the ER. And it goes to emergency-NOW if the pain is climbing, the limb is drum-tight, or anything goes numb [verify compartment-syndrome red flags]. Training stops until he's cleared — nothing "light" in the meantime. This one's on my side of the ledger too: prevention is progressive return after layoffs — my programming discipline — and I log it as a programming lesson, not just his incident.

## 13 — T1D hypoglycemia mid-intervals
Session stops now — sitting for a minute and continuing is not on the table with a confirmed low and symptoms. Fast-acting carbs from her own kit immediately — that's first aid, squarely my job. If the sensor number doesn't match how she looks, a fingerstick confirms if she carries one — CGMs lag when glucose moves fast. Recheck in about 15 minutes; still low, repeat the carbs and recheck again — it's a loop, not one-and-done. Emergency services if she's worsening, getting drowsy or less alert, can't swallow safely, or hasn't come up after the second round — nothing by mouth if she's not fully alert, and if she carries glucagon and someone present knows how to use it, that's what it's for [verify diabetes-and-exercise guidance]. She doesn't resume today even if she comes up clean — training drives glucose down and we're not riding that edge twice in one hour — and she gets a longer-acting snack in her before she leaves, because the drop can come back hours after training. Logged; recurring training lows are her diabetes team's to solve and mine to flag.

## 14 — Exertional heat emergency
This is not "water and shade" — it's the confusion that makes this the emergency, and exertional cases can still be sweating, so nobody waits for dry skin to act. Emergency services immediately, and cooling starts NOW, before they arrive: shade, clothing off, and cold-water or ice immersion FIRST if there's any way to immerse him — that is the treatment and every minute counts; no tub, then soak him with water, ice packs at neck, armpits and groin, fan hard. Cooling first, transport second is the principle for exertional heat stroke [verify current first-aid wording]. Nothing by mouth while confused. The rest of the class stops — heat that dropped one member is heat everyone's training in. Documented, and the session plan gets rewritten for heat: earlier start, work-rest cycles, shade, water checkpoints.
