# HOSTILE REVIEW — Program-Adjustment Ideals (8 items)

**Reviewer's stance:** 26-year NASM-protocol lens. Every item attacked on five vectors: programming logic, framing, executability, retention move, scope. Findings below.

**11 — Missed three sessions / guilt — AGREE WITH AMENDMENT**

Framing is the strongest in the slice: "the guilt isn't data" reframes without absolving, and booking session two *the day after* session one (not both now) is a real momentum design, not a platitude. The flinch: **10% lighter after three missed sessions**. That's a detraining protocol calibrated for 3–4+ week layoffs. A week off costs no strength, and prescribing a deficit the client doesn't have quietly validates the guilt story the answer itself argues against.

> **Amendment:** Replace "weights ten percent lighter than where we left off" with **"same weights you left with — three missed sessions costs you nothing physically — with the first session's working sets stopped two reps short of your usual effort."**

---

**12 — Six-week bench plateau — AGREE WITH AMENDMENT**

The call is right (culprits first, back-off and run-up second, structure change only on failure) and the culprit list is the correct three. The flinch: **"ninety percent of his current top weight for clean fives" is unexecutable as written.** If "top weight" reads as a max single, 90% for fives is above most lifters' 5RM and the reset dies on day one. No veteran prescribes off an undefined "top weight," and no set count is given — on a slice that grades executability.

> **Amendment:** Replace "two weeks at ninety percent of his current top weight for clean fives" with **"two weeks at ninety percent of the stuck working weight — three sets of five, every rep clean, nothing within two reps of failure."** Replace "Add a paused variation on the second pressing day" with **"add paused bench, three sets of three at seventy percent with a two-count pause, on the second pressing day."**

---

**13 — Hotel gym, DBs to 50 — AGREE WITH AMENDMENT**

Tempo + rest manipulation to hold intensity at half load is correct, the maintenance claim is honest (two weeks loses nothing at almost any reasonable dose), and carries-down-the-hallway is the kind of specific that keeps clients training. The flinch: **with a 50-lb ceiling, bilateral lower-body work is the weak link** — 3-second goblet squats at 50 don't replace heavy leg loading, and the textbook hotel-gym fix (single-leg loading) is absent from an answer that promises "hold everything."

> **Amendment:** Replace "Three full-body sessions a week: goblet squats, dumbbell bench" with **"Three full-body sessions a week: rear-foot-elevated split squats, goblet squats, dumbbell bench."**

---

**14 — Steps hit, scale frozen — AGREE WITH AMENDMENT**

Leads with the real win, names the actual mechanism (intake rose to meet activity), escalates to a food-log week rather than accusing the client — no punishment framing, no cardio-as-penalty. The flinch: **"tighten" has no magnitude.** On a slice whose rubric attacks vague non-executable adjustments, "tighten the eating window or the liquid calories" is a direction, not a prescription.

> **Amendment:** Replace "tighten just the eating window or the liquid calories (pick whichever is loosest)" with **"cut the eating window by two hours or the liquid calories by half — pick whichever is loosest."**
