## HOSTILE FINDINGS

**1. The program optimized everything except delivery. (Sequencing — fatal if continued.)**
What's wrong: The H0 gate was designed as the discipline mechanism — evidence before app work — and then the team spent the entire discipline window doing everything except starting the clock. Phases 5, 6, 7, and 8 are all, formally, work done to avoid the one gated action. That is not discipline; it is displacement with good documentation. Why it matters: the gate measures *school days*. The year began 2026-08-17. Every uninstalled day is gate evidence that can never be recovered, and T's week-one impressions of "the thing O is building" are being formed by its absence. Fix: hard rule — no artifact, design, or review work until H0 is installed and the first dump cycle has completed. Violations of this rule are what the last two days look like.

**2. Seven adversarial rounds, three models, zero users.**
What's wrong: All 13 defects were found by models reviewing against *imagined* T. The actual T has never seen the runbook, the prompt, or a printable. The single largest unreviewed defect class — "T won't do this ritual as designed" — is invisible to the review loop that found the other 13. Why it matters: the H0 gate tests T's unprompted compliance with a ritual she did not co-design. If the gate fails, nobody will be able to say whether the product failed or the choreography did. Fix: the install includes a 20-minute walkthrough *with T* where she may reword the ritual. Her edits are accepted unless they break a safety invariant. This converts the gate from a compliance test into a measurement.

**3. The fix-introduced-defect ratio is the program's true signature: 8 of 13.**
What's wrong: The review loop generates risk faster than the underlying design does. Worse: after declaring R7 dry on identical bytes with two parallel models, the team made a post-clearance additive edit and disclosed it instead of re-reviewing it — executing, immediately, the exact change class their own ledger had just identified as the most dangerous. Why it matters: the process law existed and was broken the same day it was proven. Fix: post-clearance edits are banned, full stop; the edit is reverted or the artifact is re-reviewed (one parallel pair, cheap). And a standing cap: any artifact enters a change-freeze after two consecutive clean parallel rounds — further "improvement" requires user evidence, not reviewer appetite.

**4. The coach pivot is un-gated scope change, and its "confirmed fit" is the packet's own named error class.**
What's wrong: The workstream documents — with admiration — a procedural rule that voice-sourced environment facts are [UNVERIFIED] until checked, because that error recurred five times. Then Phase 8 "confirms the fit" of the coach architecture by *reading a contract*. A document confirms the loop's shape on paper. It says nothing about whether a compressed 90-line frontier-model contract survives an 8B/14B local model, which the packet itself flags as the known risk. The confidence is asserted, not measured. Why it matters: this is how the program ends up building v2 on a premise that fails on first contact with a small model — while v0 still hasn't run. Fix: reclassify the coach port as "promising hypothesis, unverified." Its first artifact is not a spec; it is a 20-prompt behavioral test of the compressed contract on the local 14B against the held-out corpus. That test is cheap and kills the idea in an afternoon if it deserves killing.

**5. The two cheapest, highest-value facts in the packet went uncollected for two days while models debated redaction pipelines.**
What's wrong: "Where is the laptop at midday?" and "What does the parent app already record?" are each one text message. Both are structural — the first determines whether the strongest model is where the work is; the second determines whether half the S1–S12 roadmap is filling gaps or building banned duplicates. Why it matters: the program spent dollars-and-days on adversarial review of artifacts whose deployment geometry is unknown. This is effort-allocation inversion: review cost scaled with reviewer availability, not with decision value. Fix: O texts T both questions *tonight*. This takes precedence over every design thread.

**6. Nobody has ruled on whether week one of a solo preschool year is the right moment to introduce any new habit at all.**
What's wrong: T has 12 two-year-olds, no aide, and the year started yesterday. The program assumes her attention is available because the product is ready. That is a builder's assumption, not a teacher's reality. Why it matters: if H0 installs into survival-mode week one and fails, the failure will be misread as product failure and may poison the whole year-long mandate. A gate that starts when the user is ready measures the product; a gate that starts when the builder is ready measures the builder's impatience.

**7. The kill criteria have no resurrection path, and the wrong-child tripwire may fire fast.**

Fix: define the fallback now — model proposes, human confirms *every* child attribution, strikes counted only on post-confirmation errors. **8. O is the program's single point of failure and its source of uncontrolled change.**

## DISSENT

**This packet's framing is wrong in three places, and the wrongest is the one it is proudest of.**

First, "hostile-reviewed to dry, ready to install" is false comfort of a specific kind: the artifacts are dry against *model-imagined users*. Seven rounds of adversarial review by systems that have never met a preschool teacher, will never meet one, and share O's assumptions about what a teacher's day feels like, produced confidence that is orthogonal to the only open question — whether T will do the ritual. The packet presents dryness as the finish line. Dryness was the *starting* line, and the program treated reaching it as an accomplishment worth two more days of polish.

Second, the packet asks reviewers to rule on "discipline or displacement" as if the H0 gate's existence settles the discipline question. It does not. A gate the program refuses to walk through is not discipline; it is a decoration that licenses indefinite preparation. The tell is in the sequencing the packet itself reports neutrally: the gate was defined in Phase 1, and Phases 5 through 8 are all things done *instead of* walking through it — including designing the Desk for a lane with no measured traffic, adjudicating a production system that isn't T's, and pivoting to a new architecture the same week the old plan's first physical step was due. The packet's own table of contents is the dissent.

Third — and this is the paragraph the Final Decider will be tempted to cut — **the coach-architecture pivot should be suspected precisely because it is elegant.** The packet invites reviewers to ask whether it is "O's enthusiasm for his own architecture," then answers its own question by listing how beautifully the production contract matches the loop the panel designed from scratch. Of course it matches: the same mind designed both, and convergence between a person's two designs is evidence about the person, not about the problem. The correct prior is that "this IS the loop we designed" is a similarity judgment made by the party with the most to gain from it. That doesn't make the port wrong — it makes the step-6 contract test the *entire* decision, and it means no one involved in either design should be the one to grade it. The packet also never asks the rudest available question: whether "an assistant for the whole year," spoken by a teacher in August, was a request for a program at all — or for relief this month. The forward plan above is right-sized only if someone, at install, lets T answer that question out loud, and her answer is allowed to shrink the program. Nobody in two days has created a moment where that answer could be given. Create it tomorrow.
