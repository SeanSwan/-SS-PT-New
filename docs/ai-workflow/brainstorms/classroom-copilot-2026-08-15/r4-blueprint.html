Rounds 4–5 · Unlensed Panel · Contract Frozen

Classroom Copilot Blueprint

Three models reviewed the project with no assigned lenses and found six defects the lensed round never surfaced — including one that leaks the child database to Google Drive with zero code written. A second pass then reviewed the decisions made in response, destroyed one of them, and froze the event-emission contract.

Panel GLM 5.3 · Kimi K3 · HY3 Cost $0.52 across both rounds Date 16 Aug 2026 Rule 82 live on main · 8257e42b6 Status contract frozen · 3 decisions provisional · 3 questions still open

This page supersedes the round-4 model replies wherever they disagree.

Those replies are retained as evidence and still hold the fullest technical detail — but they predate the decisions and corrections below, and they still contain a 30-day expiry default, a permanent-death kill criterion, a home-Wi-Fi transfer channel that cannot exist without the network permission, and file-based encryption where SQLCipher is required. A builder must treat this page as authoritative and read the replies only for detail this page does not carry.

Did the re-run justify itself

Yes — decisively. Unlensed, the panel found defects no earlier round had named at all. Two of three models independently converged on the same structural flaw from different starting angles. That convergence is exactly the signal a lensed panel is designed to suppress, because lensing optimises for non-overlap between reviewers.

The cheapest model on the panel ($0.007) produced a legal-risk catch the expensive ones missed. The most expensive ($0.31) found the single worst defect in the project. Neither needed a lens to be useful.

The six defects that must change

Ranked by severity × likelihood × cost-to-fix-later. The rail colour separates critical from high.

D1
Kimi · unique catch

Android Auto Backup silently exfiltrates the child database

An Expo app has allowBackup on by default. Android copies the app sandbox — including copilot.db — to the teacher's Google Drive. Child data leaves the device with zero code written and no user action. Every privacy control designed across three rounds sits downstream of this one manifest flag.

Fix · android:allowBackup="false" and res/xml/data_extraction_rules.xml excluding the database path from both <cloud-backup> and <device-transfer>. Round 5 correction: on Android 12+ (API 31), allowBackup alone is ignored and fullBackupContent no longer applies — her S24 runs Android 14, so the original fix was half a fix. Verify against the merged release manifest of the built APK, not the source, and prove it with a real backup attempt — aapt dump of a flag is not evidence that nothing leaves.
D2
GLM + Kimi · converged independently

The core loop is assigned to the wrong device

Round 1 settled on-phone extraction. Rounds 3–4 locked the extraction tiers to her Mac. Both cannot be true, and nobody reconciled them. At 12:47, in a dark room with sleeping two-year-olds, the Mac is in another building — so the product's one anchored moment has no brain behind it.

The open question "where is the laptop at midday?" was never a logistics question. It is an architecture verdict wearing a logistics costume, and the answer is "nowhere useful."

Fix · T0 = Qwen3-4B Q4_K_M on the phone via llama.rn, rules-first, model optional. The Mac becomes the evening engine for work that is already evening-shaped. The open question is dissolved, not answered.
D3
GLM + Kimi

The kill criteria cannot be measured, and the history is unrecoverable

"≥90% accepted without correction" and "wrong child — zero" both require an append-only event log that appears in no schema draft. Day-one behaviour that isn't logged is gone forever. This — not the pinned flag — is the true "must exist in v1" item, because a column is a trivial migration but a missing history is a permanent hole.

Fix · RECORD_EVENT written from the first build, plus attribution_src so a model's guess is distinguishable from her own choice.
D4
GLM · unique catch

The product ends one step short of its own deliverable

The value is realised inside the mandated parent app. That handoff — draft, then paste per child, ten to fourteen times, against a pickup deadline — is the most repeated interaction in the product and appears in no brief, no slice, and no kill criterion.

If copying is clumsy she drafts in the app twice to be polite, then goes back to typing directly into the parent app — and the habit metric reads false-positive for weeks while the product quietly dies.

Fix · a Family Notes screen with per-child Copy note and — per the frozen emission contract — one HANDOFF row plus one HANDOFF_ITEM per constituent record, written on clipboard success. Format is blocked on knowing what the parent app records.
D5
GLM + Kimi

The locked input choice violates the locked privacy constraint

"Gboard's on-device dictation" is not provably on-device. It depends on per-device settings, language packs, and account state, and it falls back to network recognition silently. The project already wrote the correct rule — uncertainty always blocks — and then failed to apply it to its own keyboard.

Fix · long-form dictation moves in-app to whisper.rn, which is provably egress-free once the build carries no network permission — the same guarantee the gateway aspires to, obtained by absence instead of by classification.
D6
GLM · unique · recovery key from Kimi

There is no backup slice, and the data that never expires has no survival story

Incidents are legally protective and exempt from expiry — and a lost phone in October destroys them permanently. Slices S1 through S12 contain no backup slice at all.

Fix · encrypted backup and tested restore in S1, a hand-written paper recovery key in her classroom drawer (never photographed, never in O's vault — a non-technical user will forget a passphrase), and a quarterly restore drill.

Three decisions — provisional

These were genuine disagreements between models, now resolved provisionally so the blueprint stops carrying open branches and can be built end-to-end.

Provisional means exactly that. Each decision below is a recommendation the owner has not ratified, recorded as a working default on 16 Aug 2026 so work is not blocked on a conversation. Each carries its reversal cost. Reversing any one is a small, contained edit — the places it touches are named.

① What happens after a wrong-child error

Locked: two errors and the model path dies permanently, rules-only forever.

  • GLM — uphold it. Just define the term precisely and instrument it.
  • Kimi — it is a suicide pact. Rules-only extraction cannot resolve "the little boy who had a rough drop-off" either, so the day the model path dies, the product dies — extraction is the one feature. Proposes graduated degradation: raise the confirmation threshold, then shrink model scope, then rules-plus-manual.
  • HY3 — trigger on uncorrected wrong-child only. An error she caught and fixed during review is the system working, not failing.
Provisional decision · Kimi and HY3 are right about different halves. Count uncorrected wrong-child only, and graduate the response rather than terminate it — keeping the zero-tolerance spirit by making the first strike loud and immediate, because that spirit is what protects her.
  • Day one, unconditionally — every model-sourced attribution needs a confirmation tap, and confidence is always visible. Round 5 correction: this was originally the strike-1 penalty, which was incoherent — the same document called the tap "the real defence." A defence is not a punishment you earn by failing. It ships in S1 for all sub-explicit-name attributions.
  • Strike 1 — a loud, immediate in-app banner and model_mode = suggest_only: the model may propose but the UI never pre-fills.
  • Strike 2 — the model stops attributing children entirely. Rules-only attribution — explicit name matches and nothing else. It still classifies record types, which is most of its value and carries none of the risk.
  • Never a silent continuation, and never a total shutdown.

Round 5 correction — my earlier claim that this needed no new fields was wrong, on two independent grounds. Both reviewers destroyed it. Ordering: RECORD_EVENT.id was a uuid, so the comparison rested on a wall clock that moves backwards on NTP correction — silently reclassifying a real escape as "caught in review," the failure direction that hides harm. Granularity: a handoff was logged per child and corrections per record, so the join the rule needs did not exist in the schema at all. The strike definition now lives in the frozen emission contract above and requires HANDOFF, HANDOFF_ITEM and seq. The principle survived; the mechanism did not.

Known weakness, stated rather than hidden: this only fires if she notices and corrects the error at all. If she never notices, nothing fires. The counter under-reports by construction and is a lagging indicator, not a safety mechanism — the actual defence is the confirmation tap on low-confidence attributions, which prevents the error instead of counting it. Do not let the counter's existence justify weakening the tap.

Reversal cost — low. Touches the strike rule and the review screen's confirmation behaviour. No schema change either way.

② Whether observations should expire by default

HY3 argues that even with pinned and expiryExempt present, defaulting ordinary observations to expire is a legal time bomb: a contemporaneous note about biting that hadn't been promoted yet can be the only thing that protects her in a dispute. Proposes expiry becomes opt-in per record — she marks a note "scratch" rather than marking the important ones "keep."

GLM and Kimi both accepted the existing amendment as sufficient. This is a real fork.

Provisional decision · neither pole. The storage argument for expiry is false. Round 5 correction: my original figure was ~10× optimistic — it counted record bodies only, ignoring raw captures, several events per record, and SQLite page overhead. Corrected, five years is tens of megabytes rather than five. The conclusion is unchanged and the argument is still won; the "0.2% of one file" phrasing was rhetoric that outran its arithmetic, and it was quotable, which is worse. Indefinite retention is also wrong — for data minimisation rather than space: holding behavioural records on named two-year-olds forever is itself a worse privacy posture, and if the school states a retention expectation, "forever" breaks it.
  • Ordinary observations retain through the end of the school year plus one term — not 30 days, not forever. Open challenge from Round 5: this assumes disputes surface within ~7 months of year-end, but toddler-room transition-age disputes typically run 12–15 months out. Another full year costs single- digit megabytes. The window is being chosen on the wrong margin and should probably be longer — decide it with the school's own number.
  • Raw captures are purged with their last live record, not retained forever. The raw dump is the most sensitive artefact in the system — it holds everything she said, including fragments never turned into records. Keeping records a year while keeping their source text indefinitely was incoherent. Captures linked to an incident are permanently exempt.
  • An incident exempts its siblings. Creating an incident sets expiry_exempt on every record sharing its capture — the surrounding context ("otherwise a calm morning") is often what protects her, and nothing exempted it before.
  • Incidents and promoted evidence remain exempt. Unchanged, and correct.
  • Expiry becomes opt-in for early deletion — she marks a note scratch to drop it sooner.
  • The scratchpad feel comes from an active/archive view, not from deleting anything. Retention and visibility are separate dials.
  • Year-end rollover runs the retention pass, which is where school_year earns its place in the schema.

Superseded by the school's own number, if one exists. Question 3 of the employer conversation asks for it. If the school states a retention expectation, that wins over anything chosen here — which is why the purge job should be the last thing built, not the first.

Reversal cost — low now, unrecoverable later. Changing the window is one setting; records already deleted under a wrong window do not come back. This asymmetry is the whole argument for erring long.

③ What "no sync" should actually lock

All three say the current lock is mis-framed and makes S6 and S8 unbuildable.

  • GLM — deliberate encrypted "briefcase" export, teacher-initiated, weekly.
  • Kimi — lock "no server, no cloud, no account" instead of "no sync"; same-room end-to-end-encrypted transfer keeps data on her two devices and makes nobody an operator.
  • HY3 — an explicit local transfer path is mandatory, not optional — without it the product is two inert halves.
Provisional decision · lock this wording instead:

"No server. No cloud. No account. No background sync. A deliberate, teacher-initiated, encrypted, same-room transfer is permitted — a briefcase export, never a sync engine."

The original lock was defending two real things: the complexity of a conflict-resolution engine, and the developer never becoming an operator. Both survive intact. What it banned by accident was the one-directional export, which the conference binder and display-board slices require and cannot be built without.

The word matters. "Sync" implies continuous reconciliation and silent record loss on the device holding protective records. "Briefcase" implies she picks it up and carries it, once, on purpose.

Sequencing that makes this nearly free — with the model on the phone, v1 needs no transfer at all. The core loop is entirely phone-local. The briefcase is only needed when the Mac-side slices arrive. Decide the wording now; build the mechanism later.

Reversal cost — near zero. It is a wording change until the first Mac-side slice is built.

Data model

The event-emission contract — frozen

This is the half-page nobody had written, and it is the most urgent artefact in the project. The unrecoverable risk was never a missing column — it is the semantics of what gets emitted, when. Event history written under the wrong rules cannot be reconstructed, and the first real school day is when it starts accruing.

  1. Ordering is seq, never at_ut. RECORD_EVENT.seq is INTEGER PRIMARY KEY — SQLite's rowid, which is monotonically increasing within the table. at_ut is a device wall clock; it moves backwards on NTP correction and ties within a millisecond. Every strike, metric, and replay orders by seq. at_ut is for display only.
  2. A handoff writes one HANDOFF row plus one HANDOFF_ITEM per constituent record. The per-child grain alone cannot answer "which observations actually reached a family" — that question is the entire kill criterion. child_snapshot stores the child as sent, so a later correction cannot rewrite what history says went out.
  3. Strikes are computed, never emitted. wrong_child is deleted from the enum — it was a second event for a fact already recorded, and two sources of the same truth diverge. The definition:
    a RECORD with attribution_src = 'model' that appears in HANDOFF_ITEM, and subsequently by seq receives a child_corrected or a dismissed event.
    Post-handoff dismissal counts. A wrong attribution she deletes after it was sent is still a wrong attribution that was sent — excluding it was the under-count that hid the worst case.
  4. Undo writes a compensating event, never a deletion. The log is append-only, so the 5-second bulk-accept undo emits unaccepted and flips status back to pending. Acceptance rate is accepted − unaccepted.
  5. RECORD_EVENT.record_id carries no foreign key. Deliberately a dangling reference: the event log is the system of record for history and must survive the purge of the record body. With an FK, the retention job would erase the very evidence the kill criteria depend on. Same for HANDOFF_ITEM.record_id.
  6. The handoff event is written on clipboard-success, not on button-press — emitting before the copy actually lands records a handoff that never happened.

Acceptance test for the contract: purge a record that was handed off and then expired; assert its RECORD_EVENT and HANDOFF_ITEM rows survive intact. Advance the device clock backwards 48 hours between a handoff and a correction; assert the strike is still detected. Both are unit tests, both ship with S1.

Enum reconciliation — the document yielded to the working code

This page previously specified obs · parent · supply · task · activity · admin. A rules-only sorter already exists, is measured, and produces a different and richer vocabulary. The measured implementation wins over the document — the enum above is now the sorter's. Emitting under the wrong vocabulary is precisely the unrecoverable-history failure this freeze exists to prevent, and a document has no standing against code that runs.

Chip labels in the wireframes are display abbreviations, not enum values. The rail shows OBS and FOLLOW-UP because seven full type names do not fit a phone width; the stored values are observation and child_followup. Storing the abbreviation is the same class of error this reconciliation just fixed.

A second drift, not previously flagged: the sorter already computes triage. It emits bucket ∈ must · should · extra at sort time — which is the core output of S4, four slices later. The schema had no such field. It is added above, because a value the producer already computes must have somewhere to land or it is silently discarded on every record from day one.

Consequence for the roadmap: S4 is substantially cheaper than its 3-day estimate — the classification work is done, and what remains is the surface that displays and overrides it.

?

One open question this reconciliation surfaces — for a human, not a builder. Family-note drafts assemble from observation and parent records. Should child_followup join them? Health items often should reach a parent; behaviour items are exactly where a careless auto-draft does harm, and the project already forbids AI-written incident narratives. Deliberately not decided here. Default until someone decides: child_followup is surfaced on the family-notes screen for her to include by choice, never auto-drafted into the blob.

Must exist in v1 — because adding these later is unrecoverable

Ordered by what cannot be reconstructed. Note that column additions are trivial SQLite migrations — the real rewrite risk is missing history, not missing fields.

  1. RECORD_EVENT rows written from the first build, ordered by seq. Acceptance rate, strikes, and handoff counts are the kill criteria. Unlogged history cannot be reconstructed — and history logged without a monotonic ordering key is history whose order can never be recovered, which is the same loss.
  2. HANDOFF + HANDOFF_ITEM from the first build. Without the per-record grain, the project's own zero-tolerance criterion is not computable. Retrofitting cannot reconstruct which observations were sent on day one.
  3. CAPTURE.raw_text retained for as long as any record it produced is live. Re-extraction after a model change, and any audit, both die without it — but retaining it beyond its records contradicts the minimisation argument, and the raw dump is the most sensitive artefact in the system. Captures linked to an incident are permanently exempt.
  4. attribution_src. Strikes must distinguish a model's guess from her own choice, or the kill criterion punishes the wrong thing.
  5. pinned / expiry_exempt. Already locked.
  6. taint. One column now; a retrofit across every derived object later is the actual rewrite.
  7. school_year, join_date, leave_date, status on CHILD. Cheap now, spring-rollover-blocking later.

Do NOT build these

The things a builder will be tempted to add that would damage this product.

Product & scope

  1. No photos of children. The single most tempting addition. Biometric-class PII of minors that explodes the sensitivity of every downstream control — and the parent app already owns photos, so you'd be rebuilding the mandated system you're banned from duplicating.
  2. No stored voice audio. Transcribe and discard. A stored child's voice is an evidence liability with zero product need.
  3. No cross-device sync. Sync is a conflict-resolution engine wearing a feature costume, and its dominant failure mode is silent record loss on the device holding protective records.
  4. No iOS or macOS port. Zero second users exist. Every week spent there is a week not spent on extraction accuracy, which is the actual product.
  5. No accounts, no auth, no "tiny backend." The entire legal posture is the developer never sees a byte. One relay server touching a child record makes the developer a regulated operator.
  6. No streaks, badges, or notification nudges. Morale features must forgive the bad week; streaks punish exactly the moments she needs the tool most.
  7. No analytics dashboards. She needs flags — "C8 silent for 9 days" — not visualisations. Charts imply an archive posture that contradicts the whole design.
  8. No polished export formats early. The classic week-eater that always arrives before accuracy is proven.

Data & privacy

  1. No cloud vendor in the child-data path. "This is hard" routes upward, locally — never outward. The gateway cannot un-send.
  2. Nothing auto-sends to parents. A wrong-child message to a family is the one error with no recovery. Draft-and-she-sends is the permanent shape, not a v1 limitation.
  3. No draft may introduce a fact absent from the record. Note drafting is assembly and polish of her typed facts. Any generated sentence without a source fact is flagged or deleted.
  4. Never weaken the expiry exemptions. No "simplification" may remove pinned or expiryExempt.

Security & builder discipline

  1. Redaction is never primary. "The little boy whose mum is in hospital" passes every scrubber ever written. Classify and deny; uncertainty blocks.
  2. Never merge the vaults. The school authorised her, not him.
  3. Do not police her browser. A stricter gate makes the vendor tab more attractive. The mitigation is local quality, not surveillance of your own user.
  4. No free-form "chat with all my data." That is the prompt-injection and taint-laundering surface. The model gets typed operations, nothing else.
  5. Never auto-update models or extraction logic silently. The trust contract is "corrections stick." Invisible drift means she stops correcting.
  6. Never test with real child data on any machine but hers. Synthetic rosters everywhere else, full stop. A "quick parse test" on the 5090 is precisely the breach this architecture exists to prevent — and the one with no undo.

The principle underneath all of it

Controls must be verifiable by someone who isn't standing there.

No network beats a classifier, because you can aapt dump it. Synthetic fixtures beat a no-real-data policy, because a repo diff proves it. Events beat impressions, because SQL counts them. A paper recovery key beats a remembered passphrase, because paper doesn't depend on memory.

This is also why the round-3 gateway — however well designed — is the wrong thing to build first. In v1, the strongest boundary is an empty socket.
