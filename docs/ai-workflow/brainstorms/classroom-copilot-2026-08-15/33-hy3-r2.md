## TOP 5 ABSENCES

1. **Protective contemporaneous documentation (incident / injury / allegation / parent dispute)**
   - *Why omitted:* She is writing from inside survival mode; worst-case moments are precisely what the overloaded mind refuses to pre-plan for.    - *Which slice:* S2 · Incident Shield

2. **Emotional‑energy mirror (active morale replenishment, not just a DONE list)**
   - *Why omitted:* She named the DONE list as “most important” for workload, but treated emotional drain as a personal weakness, not a designable gap. The packet shows she wants to feel accomplishment but never asked to be *shown* it or comforted by the tool.
   - *Which slice:* S3 · Emotional Mirror

3. **Month‑3 conference & assessment prep (summative parent narrative, evidence gaps)**
   - *Why omitted:* Week‑1 blindness—conferences feel distant; she listed per‑child observations but not the invisible labor of turning 10 weeks of notes into a parent‑ready story under deadline.
   - *Which slice:* S5 · Month‑3 Bridge

4. **Substitute day / own sick‑day continuity packet**
   - *Why omitted:* Solo teachers assume they must never be absent; she has no mental model of a tool that could “cover her” because no one modeled it. The idea of exporting her brain feels like extra work, not relief.

5. **Licensing‑visit / audit read‑out (proof of compliance from local notes)**

   - *Which slice:* S5 · Month‑3 Bridge (with S2 export)

## FULL GAP LIST

### Group A — Failure‑moment gaps (ranked by protective value)
1. **Contemporaneous incident log** (see Top 5 #1) — neutral, timestamped, tamper‑evident capture.
2. **Allegation‑safe phrasing guard** — prompts that keep her words factual (“observed” not “refused”) when a parent dispute escalates.
3. **Injury‑first‑aid linkage** — quick capture of exact time, action, witness; auto‑formats the mandated accident report she otherwise hand‑writes.
4. **Parent‑dispute memory** — a private, non‑child‑linked note of what was said in a heated conversation, for her own recall.
5. **Licensing read‑only view** — one tap shows a clean, dated trail of observations, incidents, and communications.

### Group B — Emotional‑energy gaps (ranked by daily drain)
1. **End‑of‑day morale mirror** (Top 5 #2) — surfaces survived hard moments + positive C moments.
2. **Isolation breaker** — a gentle prompt: “You handled 12 toddlers solo today; here’s proof,” countering the silence of solo care.
3. **Decision‑fatigue卸载** — she listed must/should/nice, but the *experience* of dumping the day’s chaos and seeing it sorted is missing from her spec.
4. **Grief‑on‑exit** — when a child leaves mid‑year, a quiet acknowledgment, not just data expiry.

### Group C — Month‑3 invisible needs (ranked by crunch severity)
1. **Conference summary draft** (Top 5 #3) — aggregates observations into parent‑friendly narrative.
2. **Assessment evidence gap alert** — “C7 has no fine‑motor note in 6 weeks” before the deadline.
3. **Child regression note** — a way to flag “was potty‑trained, now not” without rewriting history.
4. **Difficult‑family comms draft** — warms up or firms up language for the hard talk.
5. **Character‑trait focus roll‑up** — she listed the monthly trait but not the month‑end collation of who showed it.

### Group D — Invisible manual repeats (ranked by frequency)
1. **Re‑typing same reminder into mandated comms app** — she captures on phone, then retypes in school app; no bridge.
2. **Display‑board narrative assembly** — she described the board but not the 10‑minute rewrite of observation→skill each time.
3. **Print/lamate labels** — listed as supplies, but the repeat labor of generating consistent visuals is unseen.

### Group E — Roster flux (ranked)
1. **Child joins mid‑year** — intake of allergies, routines without breaking existing flows.
2. **Child leaves** — graceful expiry and emotional closure (see B4).

## IF SHE ONLY GETS THREE MORE SLICES

**S2 Incident Shield, S3 Emotional Mirror, S4 Sub / Sick Packet.**

Why those: They address the three highest‑value emotional and failure‑moment gaps she completely omitted. S2 is the single largest absence (protective documentation) and the only thing that shields her livelihood. S3 returns daily emotional energy, which is the true sustainability metric. S4 gives her permission to be human (absence), preventing burnout collapse. Together they convert a note‑typing tool into a survival system; conference/licensing (S5) can wait because those crunches are month‑3, and roster flux (S6) is lower frequency.

## ENTERPRISE VERDICT

There is **no safe multi‑user enterprise path that preserves the local‑first, zero‑developer‑infrastructure posture**. The moment a second teacher’s child records touch shared storage the developer becomes a operator of children’s data under COPPA‑like and state licensing regimes—exactly what the settled compliance stance forbids.

**Architectural fork (if ever forced):** Only at the point a school *itself* provides a zero‑knowledge sync container (e.g., the school’s own encrypted S3 or Nextcloud) could a “centre” mode exist, with the app remaining a dumb local client and the developer never holding keys. That fork must be taken *never* unless the school signs a procurement and the dev ships a stripped build with no telemetry.

**Honest reframe of “enterprise level” for this single‑user tool:** It should mean *enterprise‑grade robustness for one*: military‑class encryption at rest, verified recoverability, tamper‑evident logs, audit‑ready read‑outs, and a professional UI that a director would respect. That is the useful reading of the owner’s intent and it is achieved by S7 without betraying the packet’s core.

## NEVER BUILD

- **Any cloud sync to developer servers** — destroys compliance posture; makes you a records operator.
- **Multi‑teacher shared database** — triggers consent, retention, breach, parental‑access law; school never procured you.
- **Auto‑send communications to parents** — her mandated app is separate; auto‑send risks wrong child, wrong tone, FERPA violation.
- **AI‑generated incident narratives** — fabrication risk in a legal‑protective context; only her factual entry + deterministic formatting allowed.
- **Behavior‑labeling leaderboards** — “positive‑reinforcement points system” sounds good but gamifying 2‑year‑olds’ compliance creates harmful records and she didn’t need it locally.
- **Push notifications during teaching hours** — interrupts the exact fragile rhythm we are trying to protect.
- **Analytics / usage telemetry** — betrays zero‑knowledge promise; even anonymous counts are a leak surface.
- **Voice transcription that leaves device** — her phone/laptop on‑device only; cloud dictation exposes child names.
- **A “social” feature to share classroom moments with other teachers** — solves a problem she didn’t have and multiplies risk.

## DISSENT

The packet (and Round 1) is still wrong on three experience‑critical points:

1. **Kill criterion is mis‑measured.** Round 1 says stop if she drafts family notes <3/5 days by week 2. But the Experience Lead evidence shows the emotional and protective slices (S2–S4) are the true retention drivers; a tool that only types notes may pass the gate yet still fail her in month 2 when the first incident hits. The gate should include “used Incident Shield or Emotional Mirror on a hard day” as a co‑equal signal.

2. **H0’s “paper triage sheet” may add load, not reduce it.** For failure moments, paper is exactly what she’ll lose or fear tampering. The experience design should let her phone capture a locked incident note *before* laptop sync, not force paper. The packet under‑weights phone as immediate protective device despite naming it Android flagship.

3. **The roadmap still sequences by feature breadth, not emotional risk retirement.** S5 (conferences) appears before S4 (sub/sick) in some readings; but the ability to be absent safely (S4) retires more daily terror than a month‑3 conference draft. I dissent that “value‑per‑day” was applied tightly enough to failure‑moment calm.

Finally, the packet treats “she did not mention protective contemporaneous documentation once” as a gap to note; I assert it is *the* central product failure if unaddressed, and S2 should be elevated to co‑equal with v1 rather than slice #2.
