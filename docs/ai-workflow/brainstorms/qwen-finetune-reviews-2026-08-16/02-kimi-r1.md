# Hostile Review — Three Local Brains Master Plan

**Verdict up front:** The plan's *instincts* are good (contract freeze gate, base-plus-prompt baseline, synthetic-only child data, tokens-out-of-weights) and its *instruments* are broken. Every acceptance gate as specified is statistically incapable of detecting the failure it exists to catch, the judge is circular with the generator, and the single most decision-relevant measurement in the whole document (the 8-second on-device budget) is scheduled *after* the spend it should gate. Fix the measurement layer before touching a GPU.

---

## S1 — CRITICAL: The gates cannot detect failure

### Finding 1.1 — Every acceptance threshold is noise-dominated. The plan will promote junk or kill good tunes by coin flip.

- **Track A:** 80 evals. A true 60% win rate vs base+prompt yields a 95% CI of roughly ±11 points — you cannot distinguish a real win from 50/50. To detect 60%-vs-50% at 80% power you need ~190+ paired judgments. And 25 of the 80 rows are grep-traps, leaving 55 informative rows.
- **Track C blinded judging:** "≥7/10 preferred" — P(≥7/10 | true preference is 50%) ≈ **0.17**. This gate *promotes a null-effect tune one time in six*. That is not a gate; it is a slot machine with extra steps.
- **"Zero brand-trap failures, zero safety regressions"** on n=10 and n≈few: a single stochastic flip passes or fails the entire run. Zero-tolerance gates on tiny n maximize variance, not safety.

**Corrected design:**
- Split evals into **dev (60 prompts, used for prompt iteration and trap tuning) and frozen eval (≥200 paired prompts for Track A, ≥30 blinded pairs minimum for Track C)**. The baseline system prompt is tuned *on dev only* and frozen before the eval set is touched — otherwise "best system prompt" either leaks (inflated baseline) or is a strawman (weak baseline), and the plan never says which.
- Promotion rule, pre-registered: win rate ≥58% **and** lower 95% bootstrap CI >50% overall, **and** CI >50% on the safety slice specifically, **and** zero banned-string hits on the *exported GGUF* (not the training checkpoint).
- Track C: n≥30 blinded pairs, require ≥20/30 (P under null ≈ 0.05), judge position-swapped.

### Finding 1.2 — The judge is circular with the generator. Fable writes the training answers, Fable (apparently) writes the eval "ideal" answers, and "Fable-tier" scores the outputs.

This is the deepest structural rot in the plan. The eval ideals' provenance is *unspecified* — §3.3 says "80 prompts with ideal answers" and never says who wrote them. If the same model family generates training targets and eval ideals and then judges, the gate measures **self-consistency of one model's taste**, not quality. The tune will "win" by regressing toward the generator's mean. Track C compounds it: you're distilling the agent ensemble's design taste into Qwen and then asking the same ensemble whether Qwen has good taste. Of course it will say yes.

**Corrected design:**
- Eval ideals for Track A safety/voice slices: **Sean-written or Sean-approved, 100%**. 200 rows of ideals is a day of Sean's time; that is the cheapest validity the program can buy.
- Eval *inputs* (especially Track B chaos dumps) generated by a **different model family** than the training-data generator, so generator idiosyncrasy can't be the held-out signal.
- Judge: pinned model + version + rubric prompt, recorded in the run card. **Human audit of ≥25 randomly sampled judgments per run; if Sean-judge agreement <80%, the rubric is redesigned before the scorecard counts.** No scorecard is valid until the judge is calibrated against the actual principal.

### Finding 1.3 — Track B defers its only real test and schedules its biggest known risk last.

Two sequencing errors in one track:

1. The plan itself quotes that the 8-second on-device budget is an "unmeasured open integration risk" — then schedules the measurement at **S8, after** dataset build (S5) and training (S6). Latency is almost entirely a function of base model + quant + hardware; **the tune won't change it.** You can measure `Qwen3-4B-Instruct` q4 on T's actual phone **this week for zero dollars**. If it blows the budget, Track B as designed is dead regardless of extraction F1, and you've saved two slices of wasted work.
2. Promotion (§4.4) happens on the Desk harness. On-device eval against T's real dumps — the only test that measures distribution match to reality — is mentioned as a possibility but is **not a promotion gate.** So the plan can promote a model that has never once touched its actual input distribution.

**Corrected design:** Move the latency measurement to a new S4.5 parallel slice ("base 4B on device, 3 quant levels, cold+warm"). Add to §4.4: *promotion requires* an on-device eval session where T scores the tuned model against her own real dumps locally, with hallucinated-attribution rate **≤1% absolute** (not merely "strictly lower than base" — base could be terrible and the tune merely less terrible) plus per-record-type F1 with pre-registered record-matching rules (type match + child attribution match + key-field overlap; report the attribution confusion matrix, not just scalar F1).

## S2 — HIGH

### Finding 2.1 — The synthetic-only privacy law is right and the plan misreads what it forbids. It constrains child *data*, not T's *voice* — and the plan therefore forgoes its best source of register realism.

Failure mode #2 ("synthetic flavor overfit") is correctly pre-registered and then mitigated with... more generator output plus a harness built by "a parallel session" — i.e., plausibly the same model family again. The obvious move is sitting unclaimed: **T dictates dumps about fictional children (C1..Cn), on her device, in her real voice, with her real run-ons and misheard words. No real child data exists in these recordings.** Export the text. That is privacy-compliant register capture, and 50–100 such dumps as *dev/eval* (never train) would be worth more than 2,000 additional synthetic rows.

**Corrected design:** Multi-generator training data (at minimum two model families, different prompt seeds, temperature jitter, and 10–15% of rows deliberately written "wrong-then-corrected" to break template lock), plus the T-voiced fictional-dump corpus as the crown-jewel eval. Also: the plan's remedy for near-zero training loss ("add noise variety") reflects a conceptual error — a templated extraction task *should* reach low loss; schema fluency is the point. The memorization check is **held-out generalization**, not loss shape. Fix §5 step 5 accordingly.

### Finding 2.2 — The plan trains enumerable rules into weights that belong in deterministic code, violating its own doctrine.

The plan's stated law is "facts mutate, weights don't migrate." Extend it one step the plan didn't: **verifiable rules go in code; judgment goes in weights.** Currently in weights: banned strings ("as an AI", "NASM-certified", "yoga"), number formatting for TTS ("3 sets of 10" not "3x10"), retired Galaxy-Swan tokens, MUI imports, hardcoded hex, `css`` wrapping. Every one of these is a **linter rule or output filter** — testable, deterministic, zero retrain cost when the rule changes. The plan even *checks* them with deterministic greps at eval time, admitting they're enumerable. Check them at inference time instead and the traps become non-events.

Worse, a self-own: the Track C failure-correction slice corrects "retired-theme citation" — which means **the retired tokens are named in the training data.** You are teaching the model the Galaxy-Swan palette in order to teach it not to say the Galaxy-Swan palette. SFT on corrections that contain the forbidden artifact is how you get a model that knows the artifact intimately.

**Corrected design:** (a) A guard/lint layer at inference (banned-string filter, hex-vs-token lint, MUI import check, TTS-number rewriter) — one afternoon of work, permanently correct. (b) Correction rows reference retired tokens *abstractly* ("any token not present in the injected theme context is invalid — request the current context") and never name them in assistant turns. (c) What remains in weights is the genuinely non-enumerable: critique voice, triage instinct, escalation judgment, refusal *shape*.

### Finding 2.3 — Track C is the weakest value proposition in the plan and the most likely to lose to its own baseline.

The entire Track C training signal is CLAUDE.md + review verdicts + design transcripts. CLAUDE.md **fits in context.** The base-plus-best-system-prompt baseline for Track C is therefore "Qwen3-Coder with the house law in the system prompt" — which is approximately the tuned model's training set read back to it. The plan pre-registers "tune loses to good system prompt" as failure mode #1 for Track A, but Track C is where that failure is *most* likely, and the plan proposes to spend SC1 (1–2 days of dataset build) before running the cheap experiment that would predict it.

**Corrected design:** Before SC1, run the 100-eval set against base+coded-context-prompt (CLAUDE.md excerpt + token rules + exemplar critiques in-context). If that baseline scores ≥80% of the rubric ceiling, Track C shrinks to a **DPO-only** pass on failure corrections (see 3.2) or is killed. Pre-register that kill criterion now, while killing is cheap.

### Finding 2.4 — SFT-only ignores the house's own DPO taxonomy, and SFT is the wrong tool for half the target behaviors.

Refusals, brand traps, "never cite retired themes," "never claim done without proof" — these are **preference** behaviors. SFT teaches what to do; it is structurally poor at teaching what not to do (see 2.2's self-own). The Teacher-Reference Vault playbook already specifies DPO splits and rubric types. The plan never uses them. The failure-corrections slices (both Track A and C) are *already chosen/rejected pairs* — the plan harvests them and then flattens them into SFT rows, throwing away the rejected half of the signal.

**Corrected design:** Phase 2 for each track: DPO (or ORPO, one Unsloth flag away) on the failure-correction pairs, gated by the same eval. Budget: half a day per track on the proven factory. This also fixes the brand-trap variance problem — preference training on 10 trap *categories* generalizes where 10 SFT-row grep targets don't.

## S3 — MEDIUM

**3.1 — Shadow-lane logging violates the plan's own privacy doctrine.** Stage 2 logs "tuned-local answers alongside production answers." Production answers contain client context. You are now writing client context to the training workstation, adjacent to the dataset factory that feeds on local files — the exact machine where "real client names, histories... never trained" is supposed to be law. **Fix:** shadow logs are redacted *at write time* by the existing sanitizer, stored in the gitignored vault, and mined for evals only through the redaction builder. State this in §8 or the "third repetition of one doctrine" paragraph is marketing over a hole.

**3.2 — Track B's headline metric is undefined.** "Extraction F1" on structured records with no stated record-matching rule is ungameable only because it's uninterpretable. Partial records, near-duplicates, uncertain-attribution correctness — all unspecified. **Fix:** pre-register the matching spec in the dataset profile before S5, including how `attribution: "uncertain"` is scored when gold is explicit (partial credit, and never full credit for guessing).

**3.3 — Accessibility slice: no eval, no acceptance criterion, category overclaim.** 120 rows (20% of a 600-row dataset) with zero held-out accessibility evals specified. And "a model that behaves accessibly makes every surface it powers accessible by default" is false — WCAG contrast, target size, and reduced motion live in the product layer no matter what the weights do. Half the listed behaviors (TTS-safe numbers, no emoji walls) are render-layer rules (see 2.2). **Fix:** either add a 30-prompt accessibility eval slice with a rubric (reading-level check is deterministic; screen-reader structure is checkable; patience patterns are judgeable) or cut the slice to a system-prompt behavior until Track A v1 is proven.

**3.4 — The contract-freeze gate has no artifact.** What freezes, exactly? A feeling? **Fix:** freeze = a versioned schema file whose hash is pinned in the `classroom-extract` dataset profile; the profile refuses to build against any other hash; a contract change triggers a diff-review that regenerates only affected slices. Also, Open Question 2's offer to generate against the *provisional* contract contradicts the gate's entire rationale — if Sean takes that option, label the output "register-probing, disposable," never "v1 data."

**3.5 — Provenance and registry gaps.** Run card must pin: base model revision hash, dataset hash, eval-set hash, judge model+version+rubric hash, Unsloth version, seed. And there is no promoted-model registry in Ollama — no champion/challenger naming, no rollback path. First bad promotion and you'll be reconstructing "which Modelfile was the good one" from memory. Half a day; do it in U5/U6.

**3.6 — Cost claims are low-balled at the bottom and the "$0 server" is a workstation.** $150/mo for 24/7 dedicated GPU serving is achievable only on spot/serverless with availability tradeoffs the plan doesn't mention; dedicated L4/A10G runs $400–800/mo. More importantly, the Stage-1/2 "$0 5090 server" is a **Windows desktop with a double-click launcher** — no uptime story, Windows Update reboots, it's Sean's daily machine, and "zero cost" ignores a 575W GPU's power draw. Stage 2 (production-adjacent shadow logging!) running on a personal workstation needs an availability and security paragraph, and it has none.

## What the plan fails to say at the program level

1. **No program-level success metric.** What does a promoted Track A model *buy*? Cost per interaction? Latency? Consistency? Privacy architecture? §8 gestures at all four and commits to none, so there's no way to notice the program succeeding at the wrong thing.
2. **No retraining/cadence story.** Behavior drift, base-model upgrades (Qwen3→next), dataset versioning policy — absent. The maintenance trap is pre-registered for *facts* but not for *the models themselves*.
3. **No capacity math for Stage 3.** "8B on a rented GPU" with zero concurrency/latency targets. One L4 serves an 8B at usable tokens/sec for maybe single-digit concurrent users; the assist-tier vision needs a number before it's a gate.
4. **Sean's approval — the actual target variable for "voice" — is never measured.** A 30-row "would Sean send this?" sample is the most valid voice eval available and costs an hour. The plan substitutes a Fable-tier judge for the principal and never notices.

# DISSENT

**1. I dissent from the praise I expect other reviewers to heap on the base-plus-best-system-prompt rule.** It will be called the plan's great rigor. As written, it is unfalsifiable theater: "best" is undefined, the prompt's provenance is undefined, the eval n can't resolve the comparison, and the judge is the generator's sibling. A virtuous-sounding baseline rule executed without dev/eval hygiene and statistical power is *worse* than no rule, because it manufactures confidence. Fix the instrument; then the rule deserves its reputation.

**2. I dissent from the expected attack on dataset size.** Some reviewer will say 600 rows is too small to matter. Wrong — for LoRA behavior shaping, 600–1,200 clean rows is plausible, even generous; style and policy tune in at that scale. The problem was never row count. It's that all the rows come from one generator's mouth and are graded by that generator's family. 600 rows from three generators plus human-authored evals beats 5,000 rows from one.

**3. I dissent from the plan's own headline that Track B is "the highest-value fine-tune in the house."** Highest-stakes, yes. Highest-value is unproven: the plan's own cited weakest assumption is T's *review behavior under pressure*, which is a human-factors problem that extraction F1 does not fix. A better extractor feeding a bulk-accepting reviewer counts a hallucinated world more efficiently. The honest claim is: Track B is the highest-value tune *conditional on the classroom product's human loop being solved*, and that condition belongs in the acceptance framing, not buried in a quote.

**4. I dissent from those who will call the synthetic-only privacy law too strict, and also from the plan's implementation of it.** The law is correctly absolute about child data and incorrectly absolute about everything adjacent to it. By reading the constraint as "nothing real ever touches training," the plan locked out T's voice — the one real thing that could legally and safely be captured (fictional children, real register). A privacy law should be a scalpel, and this one amputated the most valuable eval asset in the track.

**5. I dissent from the plan's framing of "design judgment in the weights."** What 250 critique rows actually instill is *critique voice and the current agent ensemble's taste*, distilled. That may be worth having — but call it taste-distillation with a shelf life, not "judgment." Judgment is what survives the brand evolving; the plan's own Galaxy-Swan lesson says baked-in taste is exactly what doesn't migrate. The tokens-out-of-weights rule is right; extend the same suspicion to taste, keep the blinded-eval bar high, and expect to retrain Track C more often than its siblings.

**6. I dissent on sequencing orthodoxy.** The plan sequences by track logic (A proves factory, B waits for freeze, C rides the factory). I would sequence by *risk retirement*: the three cheapest, most decision-lethal experiments — base-4B phone latency, Track C base+context baseline, and judge-vs-Sean calibration — all run in week one, in parallel, for under a day total, and each can kill or reshape a track before its dataset calcifies. The plan builds factories before buying the information that prices them.

---

**Bottom line:** Approve the factory upgrades (U1–U3, U5) and the S2a pilot. Block all three training runs until the eval layer is rebuilt: powered sample sizes, dev/eval separation, human-calibrated judge, on-device gate for Track B, baseline-first experiment for Track C, and the guard layer that moves enumerable rules out of the weights. The plan's strategy survives hostile review; its measurement apparatus does not.
