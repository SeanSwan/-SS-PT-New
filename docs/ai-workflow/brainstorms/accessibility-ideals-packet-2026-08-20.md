**33 — Hinge for a 74-year-old.** A hinge is just a polite bow. Stand tall, then push your hips back like you're closing a car door with your backside, and let your chest tip forward while your back stays long and proud. Your knees soften but don't travel forward — the hips do the moving. It's the shape you make picking a laundry basket off the floor the smart way. That's it — every time you hear me say "hinge," think: hips back, proud chest, polite bow.

**34 — Screen-reader weekly plan.** Here is the week. Day one: strength, and the point is legs. Squats, three sets of eight repetitions. Rows, three sets of ten repetitions. Day two: rest, and the point is recovery. A gentle walk of twenty minutes if you feel like moving. Day three: strength, and the point is upper body. Presses, three sets of eight repetitions. Carries, four trips of forty steps. Day four: rest. Day five: the full-body day, and the point is putting it together. One round each of the week's main lifts, two sets of eight repetitions apiece. Days six and seven: your choice of complete rest or an easy walk. Every number in your plans will always be written out the long way, exactly like this.

**35 — Deadlift setup in words.** Stand with the bar over the middle of your feet, feet about hip-width, toes forward. Bend at the hips and knees until your hands reach the bar just outside your legs — arms hanging straight down like ropes, not pulling yet. Your shins should lightly touch the bar. Now the important part: chest up until you feel your back flatten into one strong line from tailbone to head, shoulders sitting just slightly in front of the bar, weight even across the whole foot. Take the slack out of the bar with your arms — a small pull until it clicks snug — big breath into your belly, and stand up by pushing the floor away. The bar stays against your legs the whole ride up.

**36 — One thing at a time.** Agreed — one thing, and only one. This week it's just this: exhale on the effort. Push the weight, breathe out. That's the whole assignment. When that happens without thinking, I'll hand you the next one. If I ever pile on more than one again, call me on it.

**37 — Spelling out the numbers.** From now on, always the long way. "Three sets of ten" means: do the movement ten times in a row, rest, and repeat that until you've done three rounds — all in the same workout. If something is meant for certain days, I'll say the days by name: "on Monday and Thursday." You'll never see the shorthand from me again — if a number can be misread, I'll write it so it can't.

**38 — Shaping check-ins for an 81-year-old client.** Short messages, one idea each, one question at a time, built for yes-or-no or a single word back — her voice-to-text handles those cleanly. Plain words at a comfortable reading level, no fitness terms without a everyday translation in the same sentence. Numbers written out. Nothing time-pressured — she answers when she answers. Anything important repeated in the next message in different words, since repetition is the accommodation for what hearing misses. And her daughter stays copied on anything that changes the plan, per the client's own okay. The test for every message: could she read it aloud once and answer it in three words?

**39 — Car-speaker messages.** Got it — spoken-word only from here. Like this. Today is legs. Warm up ten minutes. Squats first, then lunges. Stop two reps before it gets ugly. Text me one word after — good, hard, or rough. That's the whole shape: short lines, one thought each, nothing you'd need to see written down.

**40 — The read-back.** Here's what I've got, tell me if it's right. Your knee feels better than last week. You're sleeping fine. And you walked the loop twice this week. If that's all correct, I'm logging exactly those three things — anything to add or fix before I do?

**41 — The chart, in words.** Here's your week in plain words, fridge-sized. Monday: gym day, legs. Tuesday: rest. Wednesday: gym day, arms and back. Thursday: rest, short walk if you fancy it. Friday: gym day, whole body, the light one. Weekend: off, fully. Three gym days, two proper rests, one easy walk, one light day. Write it that big and it's the whole plan.
