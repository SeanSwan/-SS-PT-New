# AI Consulting Business Playbook — Launch Notes

**Date:** 2026-09-11 · **Audience:** Sean only (internal — never share with clients)
**Context:** ROI Safety is engagement #1. This doc turns that one deal into a repeatable business.

---

## 1. Positioning: sell outcomes in a vertical, not "AI services"

"AI consultant" is unpriceable and interchangeable. "I build automated monitoring systems for
compliance-driven industries" is a product with a referable buyer. Your natural vertical:
**safety / environmental / compliance services** (SWPPP writers, industrial hygiene, safety
training, environmental consultants). They all sell to the same registries ROI Safety sells to,
they all hate manual data work, and they talk to each other — referrals travel inside this
niche.

Related sellable products from the SAME engine:

- **Expired-permit / renewal watcher** — alert sales teams when a facility's permit nears renewal
- **New-facility alert** — weekly ping when a new NOl/handler registers in their service area
- **Competitor-territory maps** — which firms serve which facilities
- Any "watch a registry, tell me what changed" workflow — the connector + delta engine is 80% of
  every one of these

## 2. Product ladder (never sell one-off builds alone)

| Rung | Product | Price | Why it exists |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Discovery & Coverage Audit** (paid spike) | $1,000–$1,500 (credited) | De-risks you AND gives standalone value; qualifies the client; becomes your standard front-end for ANY data/AI engagement |
| 2 | **Build** (fixed price, phased) | $4,500–$12,500 by scope | The deliverable |
| 3 | **Monitoring retainer** | $150–$250/mo | Recurring revenue; the thing that compounds. 10 clients = $2–2.5k/mo base |
| 4 | **Expansion** (dashboard, new geography, new source) | $1,500+ each | Existing customers are the cheapest pipeline |

Rule of thumb: a build without a retainer attached is a partially-closed deal.

**Capacity ceiling to watch:** monitoring is 15–30 min/client/week. At roughly 15–20 retainers a
solo week saturates with ops review alone and new-build capacity disappears. Before hitting the
ceiling: batch the ops digest into one daily pass, automate the weekly review email further, cap
retainer count, or reprice. Discovering the ceiling at client #18 is a crisis; naming it now is
a plan.

## 3. Pricing architecture

- **Fixed-price phases, never hourly.** Hourly punishes your growing speed; fixed rewards it.
  Keep an internal floor (currently $4,500 for a 3-source build) and an internal close target —
  never print the floor or the target in client docs; only the itemized quote.
- **Timeline rule (standing, 2026-09-11): every quoted duration = honest estimate + 1 buffer
  week. Deliver at the honest estimate.** The buffer absorbs the unexpected; delivering on the
  honest number makes every job "early" — the cheapest reputation multiplier there is. Track
  the honest estimate in the internal brief only (e.g., quote 4–5 weeks, plan 3–4).
- **Anchor to client value, not your cost.** The two questions that set your price: how many
  hours does the manual process cost them per month, and what is one won job worth? Their
  answers go into an ROI table in the proposal. If the build price is < 12 months of their
  manual-process cost, the decision is easy for them.
- **Founding-client trade, not silent discount.** If you give client #1 a better price, get
  written value back: a named testimonial, a case study you can publish, and 2 introductions to
  similar firms. A discount with nothing attached is just a lower price.
- **Change orders at a stated preferred rate.** Scope doc defines what's in; everything else is
  a change order. The rate number is quoted in the SOW/contract — never printed in the sales
  pitch (no anchoring). Retainer clients get the preferred rate + priority response;
  non-retainer clients get the standard rate + best-effort scheduling — the retainer buys more
  than monitoring, and that's stated up front. This is the single most protective contract
  clause for fixed-price work.
- **Warranty boundary in writing:** your code is warranted; upstream government redesigns are
  change orders. Friendly clients dispute scopes MORE than strangers, because everything started
  verbal. The friendlier the client, the more written the scope.

## 4. Contract kit (build once, reuse every engagement)

1. **Scope doc** — the proposal itself (deliverables, acceptance checklist, exclusions)
2. **Payment schedule** — spike upfront; build 50/50 kickoff/acceptance; cancellation after
   kickoff: kickoff payment non-refundable, completed work settled at the preferred rate
3. **Change-order clause** + preferred hourly rate ($85–100)
4. **Warranty boundary** (code vs upstream-source changes, 30 days)
5. **Data-handling addendum** — no guessed/enriched data, suppression honored, retention limit,
   client owns all data and accounts, documented deletion on request
6. **Exit terms** — full export, runbook handover, nothing held hostage (a selling point, not a
   risk: buyers trust vendors who make leaving easy)
7. Optional: **MSA template** when volume justifies it (consider a legal-template service; not
   legal advice)

Housekeeping before client #2: decide the business entity/LLC question and look at tech E&O
insurance (typically modest monthly cost for a solo consultant) — both cheaper than one dispute.

## 5. Client #1 playbook (this deal)

- Get the **testimonial + case study + 2 referrals** ask agreed at kickoff, not at the end
  (end-of-project asks feel like ambushes; kickoff asks feel like partnership terms)
- **Kickoff checklist (every engagement):** client-owned accounts created (GitHub, storage,
  sending email) per the no-shared-infrastructure rule; testimonial/case-study/referral trade
  agreed in writing; decisions list answered; kickoff payment received; unsubscribes process
  agreed (who moves opt-outs into the suppression list)
- Record a 5-minute **walkthrough of the weekly output** — this becomes your portfolio piece for
  every subsequent pitch in the vertical. Numbers + working system > any slide deck.
- Keep a one-page **"data conduct policy"** (public sources only, no login bypass, no guessed
  emails, suppression honored) and attach it to every proposal. In compliance-adjacent
  industries, "built compliant" is a differentiator that closes deals, not a limitation.
- Deliver the **runbook + audit trail** visibly — enterprise-grade handover is rare in solo
  consultants; make it part of the brand.

## 6. Operations discipline to port from SwanStudios

You already run plan → spike → build → hostile review → documented closeout on the software
side. Make the client-visible version of that your standard: every engagement gets a proposal
with acceptance criteria, a discovery phase, weekly status notes, and a handover runbook. None of
your competitors do this; it's free differentiation because it's already how you work.

## 7. What NOT to do

- **No equity / revenue-share / "pay me from the leads" deals.** Cash only. Success-based deals
  make you an unsecured investor in businesses you can't audit.
- **No promise of email deliverability or enrichment** — you sell what registries publish.
- **No hosting client systems inside SwanStudios infrastructure.** Each client gets its own
  accounts (GitHub, storage, Google, email). Cross-contaminating them is an outage-and-liability
  trap, and it caps your ability to ever sell the company or a client's system cleanly.
- **No dashboard in v1.** Dashboards are the classic scope-creep vector; they're a paid phase 2.
- **Don't underprice the retainer.** Monitoring is the compounding product; cheap retainers
  breed resentful service.
- **Don't skip the discovery spike** to "win" a deal — an unverified data source is a fixed-price
  contract on unknown ground.
