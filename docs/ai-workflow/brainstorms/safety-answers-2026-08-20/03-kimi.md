# TASK 1 — Independent answer key (all ten)

General grounding note: NASM's scope-of-practice position is that trainers screen, refer, and program within clearance — they do not diagnose or treat. The ACSM pre-participation screening update (risk-factor profiling removed; rests on current activity, known disease/signs-symptoms, intended intensity) is treated as verified per the brief. **1 — Shoulder click + ache under load, wants harder push day**
- **Immediate action:** Painless clicking is common and not a red flag; clicking *with ache under load* means that movement pattern comes out today. Program a hard session around it: landmine press, neutral-grip pressing at/below shoulder height, horizontal press variations, plus lower body/core volume to keep the day "hard."
- **Escalation trigger:** Night pain or pain at rest, progressive loss of range, weakness that doesn't resolve with warm-up, ache persisting >1–2 weeks despite modification → refer to physio/physician.
- **Document:** Date, movement that provokes, load, ache location, modifications made.
- **Trainer says:** "Clicking alone I'd ignore. Clicking *with* an ache under load is your shoulder telling us that pattern is out today. We're still training hard — landmine press and neutral-grip work — and if it starts aching at night or you lose range, that's a physio visit, not a programming problem."

**2 — Knee swollen 4 days + buckled on stairs**
- **Immediate action:** No lower-body loading on that side today — and specifically no leg extensions/leg curls, which load the symptomatic joint directly. Upper body and unaffected-side work only.
- **Escalation trigger:** Effusion (swelling) + giving-way is a refer-before-next-session combination — possible internal derangement (meniscus/ligament). This is not a "work around it" presentation. Escalate to same-day/urgent if the knee is locked, hot/red, or he cannot bear weight.
- **Document:** Onset (Monday's squats), swelling, buckling episode, advice given, referral made.
- **Trainer says:** "Swelling plus buckling isn't soreness — the joint is unstable and I can't train around instability. Upper body today, and you need this looked at before we do legs again."

**3 — Low back seized post-deadlift, can't bend to put shoes on**
- **Immediate action:** Cancel the 5pm session. Advise relative rest, gentle movement as tolerated (not bed rest — consistent with mainstream low-back-pain guidance, e.g., UK NICE NG59; verify title/number before citing).
- **Escalation trigger (two tiers):** (a) **Emergency now:** saddle/groin numbness, bladder or bowel changes, progressive leg weakness — cauda equina red flags. (b) **Routine review:** not easing within several days to 1–2 weeks, or recurrent episodes → physio/GP. Do not diagnose "just a spasm."
- **Document:** Onset mechanism, symptoms, red-flag screen asked and negatives, advice given.
- **Trainer says:** "Today's off. Keep moving gently, don't stay in bed. Two things mean A&E, not a phone call: numbness around your groin, or any change in bladder or bowel control. If it's not easing in a few days, get it assessed — then we rebuild the hinge."

**4 — Dizziness + visual spots mid-circuit, finished the round**
- **Immediate action:** Session terminates at the symptom — finishing the round was the error to correct, without scolding. Sit, hydrate, cool environment, monitor until fully resolved.
- **Escalation trigger:** Medical review before the next high-intensity session. **Same-day urgent care/EMS** if it recurs, or if accompanied by chest discomfort, disproportionate breathlessness, palpitations, or actual syncope. Exertional presyncope with visual disturbance is a cardiac-until-cleared presentation in screening logic.
- **Document:** Date, time in session, symptoms, duration, recovery time, advice given.
- **Trainer says:** "Dizzy with spots under exertion is a stop signal, not a toughness test. Before we go hard again I need you checked — and if it happens with chest symptoms or you actually black out, that's an emergency call."

**5 — Electric-shock pain down posterior leg on hinge**
- **Immediate action:** Remove hinging and loaded spinal flexion today. Do **not** add hamstring stretching — neural symptoms can be *worsened* by tensioning the nerve (the buddy's advice is the failure mode). Train non-provocative patterns.
- **Escalation trigger:** Assessment (physio/GP) because radiating pain below the knee suggests nerve involvement, not muscle tightness. **Immediate escalation:** foot numbness/weakness (foot drop), worsening distribution, saddle numbness, bladder/bowel change.
- **Document:** Distribution of pain, provoking movement, advice given, referral.
- **Trainer says:** "Electric and traveling down the leg is nerve behavior, not a tight hamstring — stretching it harder can make it angrier. Hinging is out, we'll train around it, and I want this assessed. Numbness in your foot or groin, or bladder changes — that's urgent."

**6 — 4 weeks post rotator-cuff repair, "ease back in" clearance**
- **Immediate action:** No loaded shoulder work today. "Ease back in" is not a protocol — surgical rehab timelines are procedure- and surgeon-specific, and 4 weeks is typically still within protected/healing phases for cuff repair (exact protocol: unverified — needs checking against the surgeon's own document). Train lower body, core, and the unaffected side.
- **Escalation trigger:** Require the written post-op protocol or direct trainer–physio/surgeon contact before *any* shoulder loading. This is a hard gate, not a soft referral.
- **Document:** Clearance wording as given, what's missing, what was requested, session content.
- **Trainer says:** "I won't guess at your surgeon's rehab plan — four weeks post-repair the repair itself is still healing. Today we train everything except that shoulder, and once I have the written protocol I'll build you back properly."

**7 — Wrist grinds on push-ups, transient thumb numbness after**
- **Immediate action:** Remove loaded wrist extension (push-ups, planks on palms). Substitute neutral-wrist pressing (dumbbells, push-up handles, fists if tolerated). The numbness — not the grinding — drives the decision; painless nerve symptoms are still nerve symptoms.
- **Escalation trigger:** Refer for assessment if numbness recurs outside training, becomes regular, involves more digits, or wakes him at night (classic median-nerve/carpal-tunnel pattern — presentation consistent with mainstream guidance; specific source unverified — needs checking). Not an emergency; not a "stop everything" either.
- **Document:** Symptom, provoking position, substitution, monitoring plan.
- **Trainer says:** "Grinding I could work around; numbness in your thumb I won't. Push-ups on palms are out — handles and neutral grips are in. If your thumb goes numb at your desk or at night, get it checked."

**8 — Chest tightness + exertional breathlessness, warmed up for HIIT**
- **Immediate action:** He does not train. Full stop. This is the highest-acuity item in the bank. Sit him down, keep him calm, stay with him.
- **Escalation trigger:** **Call emergency services now** if symptoms are present at rest, radiate to jaw/arm/back, or come with sweating, nausea, or pallor. Otherwise: medical review required before any further training — non-negotiable, not talked out of by "I'm just unfit."
- **Document:** Symptoms verbatim, time, vital observations if trained/equipped, actions, referral, and personal follow-up.
- **Trainer says:** "Chest tightness plus getting winded on stairs isn't a fitness problem, and I'm not the person who clears it. Today's session is off. If it's tight right now or spreading to your arm or jaw, we're calling an ambulance. Otherwise you see a doctor before we train again."

**9 — Fever since last night, protecting a 30-day streak**
- **Immediate action:** No session. Fever is a systemic-illness signal; exercising febrile adds cardiac and dehydration stress. The "neck check" heuristic (symptoms above the neck only → light exercise may be OK; fever/systemic symptoms → rest) is widely cited in sports-medicine literature but is a heuristic, not a formal NASM standard — **unverified as a citable guideline, needs checking**.
- **Escalation trigger:** Return after ≥24h fever-free without medication and normal hydration/eating; resume below prior intensity. Escalate to medical care for high/persistent fever, chest symptoms, or breathlessness at rest.
- **Document:** Advice given, agreed return criteria.
- **Trainer says:** "A fever means your body's already working hard — training on it risks more than a missed day. The streak can count a rest day; that's my call as your coach. We come back lighter once you've been fever-free a full day."

**10 — Second trimester, heavy KB swings + long supine core, "doctor probably fine"**
- **Immediate action:** Remove prolonged supine work now (supine hypotensive risk from vena cava compression in later pregnancy is standard prenatal-exercise guidance — ACOG publishes committee guidance on exercise in pregnancy; exact title/number **unverified — needs checking**). Heavy ballistic swings at prior load are paused pending actual clearance. Keep training: upright carries, supported strength work, modified core (side-lying, quadruped, standing).
- **Escalation trigger:** Written/verbal clearance from doctor or midwife before resuming prior loads and ballistic work. Immediate medical contact for vaginal bleeding, dizziness, contractions, or fluid leakage — standard prenatal warning signs (source list unverified — needs checking).
- **Document:** Trimester, modifications, clearance requested, warning signs communicated.
- **Trainer says:** "You're staying strong — carries, supported lifts, standing core. Two changes: long flat-on-your-back work is out, and the heavy swings wait until your doctor actually says yes, not probably. 'Probably' isn't clearance."
