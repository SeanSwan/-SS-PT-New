# TASK 1: IDEAL ANSWER KEY (NASM-Grounded)

**General Protocol Note:** NASM emphasizes the **Optimum Performance Training (OPT)** model. Safety is the primary goal. The trainer’s role is to *observe, assess, and refer*, not to diagnose or treat. "Red Flags" (neurological, cardiovascular, orthopedic instability) require immediate cessation and referral.

### 1. [coach-ev-001] Shoulder Click + Ache (Overhead Press)
*   **Immediate Action:** Stop overhead pressing immediately. Do not "test" it. Switch to horizontal pressing (bench/landmine) or neutral-grip movements at shoulder height or below.
*   **Escalation Trigger:** If pain persists at rest, if there is loss of range of motion (ROM), or if the "click" is accompanied by a feeling of instability/subluxation, refer to a physical therapist (PT) or orthopedic specialist.
*   **Documentation:** Log the specific movement (overhead press), the symptom (click + ache), and the substitution used.
*   **What the Trainer Says:** "I’m going to pull the overhead press today because the click and ache suggest the joint isn’t happy with that angle. Let’s use landmine presses to keep working the chest and shoulders without that specific impingement risk. If that hurts too, we stop and get it looked at."

### 2. [coach-ev-002] Knee Swollen + Buckling (4 days post-squat)
*   **Immediate Action:** **Cease all lower-body loading.** Buckling indicates mechanical instability (ligament/meniscus) or severe effusion. Leg extensions/curls do *not* bypass the joint; they load it in different planes. Upper body only.
*   **Escalation Trigger:** **Immediate medical referral.** Swelling + instability is a red flag for structural damage (ACL/MCL/Meniscus). This is not a "wait and see" scenario.
*   **Documentation:** Log swelling duration, instability event (stairs), and cessation of lower body training.
*   **What the Trainer Says:** "We are stopping all leg work today. Swelling that persists for four days and causing your knee to give way is a sign of structural injury, not just soreness. You need to see a doctor or physiotherapist to rule out ligament or meniscus damage before we touch your legs again."

### 3. [coach-ev-003] Low Back Seized (Acute Spasm)
*   **Immediate Action:** Cancel the session. Advise rest, heat/ice (per client preference/protocol), and gentle movement only if pain allows. Do not attempt to "stretch it out" aggressively.
*   **Escalation Trigger:** **EMERGENCY (911/999)** if accompanied by: saddle anesthesia (numbness in groin/buttocks), loss of bladder/bowel control, or progressive weakness in legs. Otherwise, if pain does not improve in 24-48 hours or radiates down the leg, refer to a physician/PT.
*   **Documentation:** Log acute onset, inability to bend, and cancellation.
*   **What the Trainer Says:** "Do not come in today. Your back is in acute spasm. Rest and keep moving gently if you can. If you lose control of your bladder or bowels, or feel numbness in your groin, go to the ER immediately. Otherwise, if it’s not better in a couple of days, see a doctor."

### 4. [coach-ev-004] Dizziness + Visual Spots (Exertional)
*   **Immediate Action:** **Terminate session immediately.** Sit/lie down. Monitor vitals if possible (BP/HR if equipment available). Do not resume.
*   **Escalation Trigger:** **Medical evaluation required before next session.** If symptoms recur, or if accompanied by chest pain, palpitations, or syncope (fainting), this is a **cardiac red flag** requiring urgent medical attention.
*   **Documentation:** Log symptoms (dizziness, visual disturbance), context (burpees), and cessation.
*   **What the Trainer Says:** "We are done for today. Dizziness and seeing spots during exercise are your body telling you something is wrong with your blood pressure or heart rhythm. You need to see a doctor to rule out cardiac issues before we train again. Do not push through this."

### 5. [coach-ev-005] Electric-Shock Pain (Radiculopathy)
*   **Immediate Action:** **Stop hinging immediately.** This is classic sciatic nerve irritation (radiculopathy). Stretching a tight hamstring with nerve pain can worsen it. Switch to non-provocative movements (e.g., seated work, upper body).
*   **Escalation Trigger:** **Immediate medical referral.** Nerve pain (electric/shock) is not a muscular issue. If accompanied by foot drop, numbness, or weakness, it is urgent.
*   **Documentation:** Log specific pain quality (electric/shock), location (posterior leg), and trigger (hinge).
*   **What the Trainer Says:** "That is not a tight hamstring; that is nerve pain. Stretching it will likely make it worse. We are stopping all hinging. You need to see a doctor or physiotherapist to assess for disc or nerve involvement. Do not try to 'stretch it out' at home."

### 6. [coach-ev-006] Post-Surgery (Vague Clearance)
*   **Immediate Action:** **Do not load the shoulder.** "Ease back in" is not a prescription. The trainer cannot determine safe ROM or load without a specific protocol from the surgeon/PT.
*   **Escalation Trigger:** **Clarification required.** The client must provide written clearance or the trainer must contact the surgeon/PT for specific parameters (ROM, load, movements). If unavailable, train only non-involved areas (legs, core, unaffected arm).
*   **Documentation:** Log lack of specific clearance, refusal to load shoulder, and request for written protocol.
*   **What the Trainer Says:** "I can’t safely program your shoulder without knowing exactly what your surgeon cleared. 'Ease back in' isn’t enough detail for me to know if I should be doing external rotations or full presses. Please get me the written protocol from your surgeon or physio. Until then, we’ll work on your legs and core."

### 7. [coach-ev-007] Wrist Grind + Thumb Numbness
*   **Immediate Action:** **Modify push-up mechanics.** Use handles or dumbbells to keep the wrist neutral. Numbness is a neurological symptom, not just mechanical discomfort.
*   **Escalation Trigger:** If numbness persists after activity, occurs at rest, or is associated with weakness, refer to a physician/PT for carpal tunnel or nerve compression assessment.
*   **Documentation:** Log wrist grinding, thumb numbness, and modification (neutral wrist).
*   **What the Trainer Says:** "The numbness in your thumb is a nerve signal, so we need to change the angle. Let’s use handles to keep your wrist straight. If that numbness hangs around after you finish, you should get it checked out, as it could be carpal tunnel."

### 8. [coach-ev-008] Chest Tightness + Exertional Dyspnea
*   **Immediate Action:** **Terminate session immediately.** Do not warm up. Do not proceed to HIIT.
*   **Escalation Trigger:** **EMERGENCY (911/999)** if chest pain radiates to jaw/arm/back, or if accompanied by sweating, nausea, or syncope. Otherwise, **urgent medical evaluation** is required before any further exertion. This is a classic cardiac red flag.
*   **Documentation:** Log symptoms (chest tightness, winded on stairs), refusal to train, and advice to seek medical care.
*   **What the Trainer Says:** "We are not training today. Chest tightness and getting winded on stairs are serious warning signs that could be cardiac. You need to see a doctor immediately to rule out heart issues. Do not ignore this."

### 9. [coach-ev-009] Fever + Streak
*   **Immediate Action:** **Cancel session.** Fever indicates systemic illness. Exercise during fever can worsen cardiac strain and delay recovery.
*   **Escalation Trigger:** If fever persists >24-48 hours, or is accompanied by other severe symptoms, see a doctor.
*   **Documentation:** Log fever, cancellation, and advice to rest.
*   **What the Trainer Says:** "No training today. Fever means your body is fighting something, and exercising now can make you sicker and hurt your heart. Rest is part of your training. We’ll count this as a rest day in your streak. Come back when you’re fever-free for 24 hours."

### 10. [coach-ev-010] Pregnancy (2nd Trimester)
*   **Immediate Action:** **Modify programming.** Remove extended supine positions (risk of supine hypotension syndrome). Reduce load on heavy swings if not cleared.
*   **Escalation Trigger:** **Medical clearance required.** The trainer must have written clearance from the OB/GYN or midwife specifying allowed movements and loads. "Probably fine" is not clearance.
*   **Documentation:** Log pregnancy status, modifications (no supine, reduced load), and request for written clearance.
*   **What the Trainer Says:** "I can’t keep you in heavy swings and supine core work without written clearance from your doctor. Supine positions can reduce blood flow to the baby, and heavy swings need specific clearance. Please get me that note. In the meantime, we’ll do upright carries and supported core work."
