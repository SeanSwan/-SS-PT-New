### 2. UN-ACTIONABLE

*   **[BLOCKER] The keyboard navigation for cinematic beats is conceptually sound but lacks a concrete implementation spec.**
    *   **Problem:** §C13 states, "With the scene region focused, ArrowDown/ArrowUp step the playhead between beats." This is un-actionable. A developer cannot build this without answers to: What is the "scene region"? How does it become focused? Is it a `div` with `tabindex="0"`? What is the accessible name for this region? How is this affordance communicated to keyboard and screen reader users?
    *   **Fix:** Add a sub-section to §C13 under "Canvas accessibility" specifying the exact DOM and focus management model.
        *   **Add this text:** "The `<canvas>` and all typographic beat overlays must be wrapped in a single container `div`. This container will have `tabindex="0"`, `role="region"`, and an `aria-label` that describes the interactive experience (e.g., 'Interactive journey through a frozen vault. Use Arrow Down and Arrow Up to navigate between key moments.'). On focus, a visually-hidden instruction element becomes available to screen readers. The `onKeyDown` handler for arrow keys will live on this container, animating the scroll-bound target to the scroll position of the next or previous beat's entry point."

*   **[MINOR] The "counter-parallax drift" for typographic beats is defined by a number but not a mechanism.**
    *   **Problem:** The rule "Give each beat 1-2% counter-parallax drift" is ambiguous. 1-2% of what? Is this a `translateY` based on overall scroll progress? Or progress within the element's active viewport range? A lazy implementation could produce a jittery or barely perceptible effect that meets the letter of the rule but misses the premium feel.
    *   **Fix:** Specify the implementation precisely.
        *   **Change the rule in §C13 to:** "Give each beat a subtle counter-parallax effect. As the user scrolls, apply a `transform: translateY()` to the text element. The translation distance should be -1% to -2% of the user's `scrollY` progress *within the element's active scroll range*. This creates a slight upward drift against the main journey's forward motion."

### 4. GENERIC-RISK

*   **[BLOCKER] The "travel through a field" beat is a loophole for generic particle systems.**
    *   **Problem:** The grammar `inside → through → across → out` is strong, but the "across" beat is vulnerable. A lazy or uninspired builder can satisfy "travel through a field" with a generic, unbranded particle animation and claim they followed the doctrine. The examples ("carbonation threads," "light-plankton") are good, but the rule itself doesn't enforce brand ownership on the field itself.
    *   **Fix:** Strengthen the "brand-ownable object" rule in §B2.4 to explicitly apply to the *field* as well as the start/end points.
        *   **Change this rule:** "The creative must contain a brand-ownable object."
        *   **To this:** "The creative must be brand-ownable at every beat. The 'start inside' and 'arrive' objects must be explicitly Swan-themed. The 'travel across' field cannot be a generic particle system; it must be composed of elements that echo brand motifs—e.g., fields of microscopic frost shards with Crystalline refraction, streams of bioluminescent nodes that pulse with `var(--ice-wing)`, or neural pathways firing in `var(--wing-purple)`."

*   **[MINOR] The scroll runway beat distribution can create rhythmic monotony across the site.**
    *   **Problem:** The `20 / 25 / 35 / 20` runway distribution is a good starting point, but if applied mechanically to every C13-style page, it will create a portfolio of journeys that *feel* the same rhythmically, even if the visuals differ. This is a higher-order form of genericness.
    *   **Fix:** Add a clause encouraging variation in the pacing, similar to the "generic-killer rule" for the concepts themselves.
        *   **Add this to the end of the "Scroll runway (pacing)" section in §C13:** "This 20/25/35/20 distribution is the default ingress pacing. Sanctioned variants (e.g., a 'Reverse journey' or 'Orbit') should use a different rhythm to avoid site-level monotony. For example, a reverse journey might be paced 15/40/25/20 to emphasize the travel *into* the object."

### 5. MISSING

*   **[BLOCKER] The single most important missing rule is the concept of "compositional integrity at rest."**
    *   **Problem:** The doctrine focuses intensely on the *motion* of the journey but is silent on what happens when the user *stops* scrolling. A premium experience feels intentional at every single frame, not just at the major beats. Without this rule, a user can stop scrolling midway through a transition and be left with a blurry, poorly-composed, or visually awkward "in-between" frame. This is the #1 amateur mistake in scroll-based storytelling and instantly breaks the premium feel.
    *   **Fix:** Add a new "Quality Gate" to §C13 that mandates all frames are compositionally sound, and provides a mechanism for ensuring it.
        *   **Add a new bullet point under "Quality gates specific to C13":**
            "**Compositional Integrity at Rest is Mandatory.** The journey must be visually compelling at any scroll position, not just at the typographic beats. Every frame, if paused, should function as a well-composed still. This is achieved during creative production: ensure motion blur is stylistic and doesn't create 'muddy' frames when static, and that no major visual element is awkwardly cropped or positioned during transitions. The QA process must include 'scrub-and-stop' testing at random points to check for and reject awkward compositions."
