### 1. Design Gaps

Your plan is missing the entire frontend design and UX specification. Key gaps include:

*   **The "Order Prints" Affordance:** The plan mentions un-gating a "Coming Soon" card but fails to define its active, interactive state. How does it invite interaction and communicate value?
*   **The `PrintStore` Experience:** The core user-facing component, `PrintStore.tsx`, is treated as a black box. There is no specification for its layout, interaction model (modal vs. drawer), or component hierarchy.
*   **Product Selection UI:** How does a user select between a print, canvas, or metal print? How are these options presented to feel premium and distinct?
*   **Configuration Controls:** The UI for selecting size and quantity is undefined. Generic browser dropdowns or input fields will not meet our premium standard.
*   **Price & Checkout Summary:** The display of pricing, including the breakdown of product cost, shipping, and taxes, is not designed. The final call-to-action (CTA) is unspecified.
*   **Admin Fulfillment View:** The plan lists data fields but provides no layout, data visualization, or interaction design. An admin's workflow efficiency depends on this. How are statuses visually differentiated for quick scanning?
*   **Animation & Interaction:** The plan lacks any mention of motion design. How do elements enter, exit, and respond to user input? This is non-negotiable for a premium feel.
*   **Accessibility (A11y):** There is no mention of focus management for the modal/drawer, ARIA roles for custom controls, or keyboard navigability. This must be designed in from the start.
*   **Responsive Behavior:** The plan does not specify how the print store or admin view will adapt across our 10 breakpoints, particularly the transition from mobile tap-centric UI to desktop hover-and-click UI.

### 2. Enhancement Opportunities

To elevate this from a simple e-commerce form to a Crystalline Swan experience:

*   **Confidence-Building Preview:** Address the un-watermarked asset risk (§5) by turning it into a UX feature. In the `PrintStore`, display a high-fidelity, un-watermarked preview of the selected image. Add a subtle text overlay: *“Your final print will be delivered in pristine, un-watermarked quality.”* This builds trust and justifies the premium price.
*   **Tactile Product Selection:** Instead of a dropdown, we will use interactive cards for product types (Print, Canvas, Metal). These cards will have a subtle parallax hover effect on desktop and a crisp, glowing border when selected, making the choice feel significant.
*   **Gamified Stepper Controls:** For quantity, we will use custom-designed "+" and "-" buttons that have a satisfying, game-like interaction on press, with a subtle glow and scale transform.
*   **The "Vault" Checkout Drawer:** The checkout summary should slide in from the right on desktop (or bottom on mobile) like opening a secure vault. The background will use our `Graphite` surface color, feeling distinct from the main page.
*   **Status Badges:** In the admin view, order statuses (`paid`, `processing`, `shipped`) will not be plain text. They will be distinct, pill-shaped badges with icons and colors for at-a-glance comprehension, inspired by the clarity of Stripe's dashboard UI.
*   **Luxury Accent Usage:** The `Gilded Fern` color will be used sparingly but effectively. A small, gilded badge on the product selector for "Metal Print" can denote it as the most premium option. The final total in the price summary will also use this color to draw the eye.

### 3. Architecture Recommendations

*   **Component Structure:** Decompose `PrintStore.tsx` into a clear hierarchy:
    *   `PrintStoreDrawer` (The main container, handles state and animation)
    *   `ProductPreview` (Displays the un-watermarked image)
    *   `ProductTypeSelector` (Contains `ProductTypeCard` components)
    *   `SizeSelector` (A custom, styled dropdown or radio group)
    *   `QuantityStepper` (The custom +/- control)
    *   `PriceSummary` (Displays line items and total)
    *   `CheckoutButton` (The primary CTA)
*   **Styled-Components:**
    *   Use the `theme` object for all styling. No raw hex codes in component files.
    *   Leverage component `variants` for states (e.g., `<ProductTypeCard selected>`).
    *   Use the `attrs` constructor for passing down dynamic HTML attributes like ARIA states to avoid cluttering the component logic.
*   **Animation Strategy:**
    *   **CSS Transitions:** Use for simple state changes like hover effects (color, box-shadow, transform). They are highly performant.
    *   **Framer Motion:** Use for layout animations, specifically for the enter/exit animation of the `PrintStoreDrawer`. It simplifies managing mounting/unmounting animations in React. The `AnimatePresence` component is perfect for this.
