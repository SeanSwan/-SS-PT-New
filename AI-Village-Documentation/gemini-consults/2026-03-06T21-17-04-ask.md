Hello Claude. Excellent questions. This is exactly the kind of complex, high-stakes feature where our partnership shines.

---

### 1. UX Approach: The Biomechanics Studio (Split-Pane Wizard)
**Reject:** Generic multi-step forms or overly complex node-based drag-and-drop graphs (too much cognitive load).
**Adopt:** A **Split-Pane Live Workspace**. 

*   **Left Pane (The Logic):** A 4-step vertical accordion wizard.
    1.  *Metadata & NASM Mapping:* Name, Category, Kinetic Chain Checkpoints.
    2.  *Rep Mechanics:* Primary joint selection (visual SVG body map), ROM (Range of Motion) thresholds for rep counting.
    3.  *Form Rules:* Block-based rule builder (more on this below).
    4.  *Review & Publish.*
*   **Right Pane (The Sandbox):** A persistent, sticky video player/webcam feed. As the trainer adjusts rules on the left, the MediaPipe skeleton overlay on the right updates in real-time to reflect the thresholds.

### 2. Storage Architecture: PostgreSQL + JSONB
**Reject:** JSON files on disk. This breaks relational integrity, makes tenant isolation (trainer-specific exercises) a nightmare, and complicates backups.
**Adopt:** PostgreSQL with a `JSONB` column for the rule schema.

*   **Why:** We need relational links to `TrainerId`, `NASMCategoryId`, and `AnalysisHistory`. The `JSONB` column allows us to store the highly flexible, heavily nested rule definitions without constantly running schema migrations.
*   **Claude, implement this schema:**
    `CustomExercises` table: `id` (UUID), `trainer_id` (UUID), `base_template_id` (UUID, nullable), `name` (String), `nasm_category` (Enum), `version` (Int), `is_latest` (Boolean), `mechanics_schema` (JSONB).

### 3. Rule Engine UX: Block-Based "If-This-Then-That" Templates
**Reject:** A blank-canvas visual node builder. Trainers are not software engineers.
**Adopt:** **Predefined NASM-Aligned Rule Blocks**.

Trainers will click "Add Form Rule" and select from NASM Kinetic Chain Checkpoints:
*   *Foot/Ankle* (e.g., Heel lift, Foot turn-out)
*   *Knee* (e.g., Valgus, Varus)
*   *LPHC* (e.g., Anterior pelvic tilt, Asymmetrical shift)
*   *Shoulders/Thoracic* (e.g., Rounding, Elevation)
*   *Head/Neck* (e.g., Forward head)

**The UI Flow:**
1. Trainer selects: `[Knee] -> [Knee Valgus]`
2. A sleek glass block appears in the list.
3. Inside the block, they adjust a **Threshold Slider** (e.g., `Hip-Knee-Ankle angle < 165°`).
4. They define the **Severity** (Minor/Major) via a custom toggle.
5. They type the **Audio/Visual Cue** (e.g., "Drive your knees outward").

### 4. MediaPipe Validation: The Live Calibration Sandbox
We cannot expect trainers to guess joint angles. 
*   **The UX:** In the Right Pane, the trainer uploads a 5-second reference video of themselves doing the movement (or uses their webcam).
*   **The Magic:** MediaPipe runs in the browser. We overlay the Swan Cyan (`#00FFFF`) skeleton. 
*   When the trainer adjusts the "Squat Depth" slider to `90°` on the left, a visual arc on the video's knee joint dynamically updates. If the trainer in the video hits `88°`, the arc flashes Cosmic Purple (`#7851A9`) to indicate the rule successfully triggered. 
*   *Validation Gate:* The system will not allow publishing until the trainer has "Tested" the exercise, ensuring the primary rep-counter logic fires at least once in the sandbox.

### 5. Versioning Strategy: Append-Only Immutable Records
**Reject:** Updating existing exercise rows. This will corrupt historical client data. If a trainer changes the squat depth threshold today, a client's perfect score from last month shouldn't suddenly recalculate to a failure.
**Adopt:** **Append-Only Versioning**.

*   When a trainer edits an active custom exercise, Claude, you will `INSERT` a new row.
*   Increment the `version` (e.g., v2).
*   Set `is_latest = true` on the new row, and `is_latest = false` on the old row.
*   All historical `AnalysisSessions` retain their foreign key to the specific `exercise_id` (v1) they were evaluated against.

### 7. Multi-Device Experience Strategy
Creating a biomechanics rule engine is a complex task. We must be realistic about device contexts.

*   **Desktop & Large Tablets (1024px - 3840px):** Full "Biomechanics Studio" experience. Split-pane layout. Left side rules, right side sticky video sandbox.
*   **Standard Tablets (768px - 1023px):** The right pane (Video Sandbox) becomes a sticky Picture-in-Picture (PiP) floating window in the bottom right corner, allowing the left pane to take up 100% width for easier touch manipulation of sliders.
*   **Mobile (320px - 767px): READ-ONLY / QUICK TWEAK MODE.** 
    *   *Design Authority Ruling:* We **do not** allow creating an exercise from scratch on a phone. It is a miserable UX that leads to errors.
    *   On mobile, trainers can *view* their custom exercises, duplicate them from templates, and tweak basic numerical thresholds (e.g., changing 90° to 85°). 
    *   The UI becomes a simple vertical stack. The video sandbox is hidden behind a "Preview" modal button.
